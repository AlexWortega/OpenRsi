I use the following obstruction ledger in every check:

- **O1 (G1):** free slack annihilates amplified residuals.
- **O2 (G2–5):** local affine/Graver isolation fails under overlap.
- **O3 (G6):** external filters, changed references, or modular bypass invalidate CVP evidence.
- **O4 (G7):** exact signed-selector kernels defeat linear/radix amplification.
- **O5 (G9/11/13):** constant-cost parity and honest-affine-span collisions defeat bounded moments and compatible raw linear codes.
- **O6 (G12):** clause dropping defeats equal-radius fingerprints.
- **O7 (G14):** pair bags pass only a constant shell, with no growing-gap law.
- **O8 (G15):** affine combinations commute through linear hierarchies; full-assignment ancestors are exponential.

## 1. Splitter-isolated logarithmic bags

**Core trick.** Use an explicit splitter/perfect-hash family on variables (Naor–Schulman–Srinivasan, 1995). Each scope has \(O(\log n)\) variables and therefore polynomially many assignment selectors; the family should isolate the variable support of every short signed attack in at least one bag, where an orthogonal-simplex anchor charges it.

**Expected move.** Turn bounded anchor support into a polynomial number of charged bags, potentially yielding \(n^\alpha\) excess energy.

**Obstruction check.** O1: no slack. O2: globally overlapping scopes, not private clause rows. O3: every selector, row, and tag is emitted. O4: zero residual is still possible but should pay bag anchors. O5: **not outside** affine lifting; the G13 combination can lift to every bag. O6: replicated normalization may charge drops, but must be tested. O7: logarithmic scopes strictly extend pairs. O8: polynomial size avoids the exponential root, but affine commutation remains.

**Falsification.** The G13 lift or one dropped scope stays within a constant factor of baseline.

**Smallest experiment.** On the nine-clause instance, enumerate all 3- and 4-variable splitter scopes, optimize rational bag weights by LP, then exact-search through \(B+64\).

**Likely death.** Every affine lift pays only a constant factor per bag.

## 2. Fixed-degree Veronese–Construction-A lift

**Core trick.** Enlarge an assignment to its squarefree monomials through fixed degree \(d\), using color-coded constant-size tables so all relations remain linear in lifted selectors. Apply a high-distance Construction-A code to these lifted coordinates, rather than hashing the 72 raw selectors.

**Expected move.** A harmful raw affine combination should require either a nonzero coded syndrome or a lifted coefficient vector with polynomially larger norm.

**Obstruction check.** O1: no free slack; any modular carries must be anchored. O2: features are global, not locally isolated. O3: lifted variables and carries are actual CVP coordinates. O4: exact lifted kernels remain possible and are the first audit. O5: outside the **raw-selector/degree-\(\le3\)** assumptions, but not the general affine-collision phenomenon. O6: code normalization can charge dropped blocks, subject to audit. O7: richer than pair bags. O8: \(n^{O(d)}\) size avoids full assignment nodes; affine lifts may still survive expensively.

**Falsification.** A degree-\((d+1)\) cube derivative has zero syndrome and constant anchor excess.

**Smallest experiment.** Set \(d=4\) on the four-variable obstruction, emit every monomial and maximal compatible code over \(p=5\), and exact-search through the G14 shell \(B+32\).

**Likely death.** Fixed degree merely moves parity to degree \(d+1\); growing \(d\) destroys polynomial size.

## 3. Truncated Nullstellensatz dual lattice

**Core trick.** Replace selector consistency by the global polynomial ideal generated by Boolean equations \(x_i^2-x_i\) and clause-unsatisfaction polynomials. Build the degree-\(d\) Macaulay matrix and use its row/dual lattice so a satisfying evaluation gives a close vector, whereas an unsatisfiable instance must represent \(1\) through a quantitatively costly certificate.

**Expected move.** Convert a lower bound on integral certificate norm—not merely degree—into Euclidean CVP distance.

**Obstruction check.** O1: no clause slack. O2: one global ideal, so local overlap assumptions do not apply. O3: the complete Macaulay matrix and fixed target are emitted. O4: signed syzygies replace signed selectors and require exact kernel search. O5: raw-selector affine collisions are outside this coordinate system. O6: no equal-radius clause tags. O7: no pair-bag shell. O8: no assignment hierarchy, though size is \(n^{O(d)}\).

**Falsification.** A low-norm rational/integer Nullstellensatz certificate exists, or required degree grows with \(n\).

**Smallest experiment.** For the nine-clause obstruction and satisfiable control, build degree \(d=2,3,4\) Macaulay matrices in Sage, compute SNF/LLL data, and solve the resulting exact CVPs.

**Likely death.** Known hard formulas may need linear degree, making the matrix exponential; certificate norms may also point in the wrong direction.

## 4. Assignment-dependent expander/Graver syndromes

**Core trick.** Couple clause selectors to an auxiliary Tanner-codeword \(y(x)\) through \(Hz-Gy=0\), allowing different honest assignments to have different syndromes. Choose simultaneous-prime expander checks so every nonzero integer Graver circuit of the combined matrix has polynomial support, following the expander-code paradigm of Sipser–Spielman (1996).

**Expected move.** Any zero-residual harmful vector must alter \(N^\alpha\) anchored coordinates; any nonzero residual receives coded distance \(\Omega(N)\).

**Obstruction check.** O1: carries are anchored, though they may recreate the slack defect. O2: the matrix is globally Tanner-coupled, not private-row composition. O3: no filtered constraints. O4: directly targets short integer kernel circuits. O5: assignment-dependent syndromes evade G13’s “all honest encodings share one target” assumption; nevertheless affine combinations can lift into \(y\). O6: a dropped clause should create expanding syndrome. O7: no pair bags. O8: bounded-degree and polynomial-size, but affine lifting remains a threat.

**Falsification.** Find a short integer circuit, especially one projecting to the G13 parity or a clause drop.

**Smallest experiment.** Attach random/exhaustive 3-regular Tanner matrices over \(p=2,3,5\) to the nine-clause selectors and enumerate all kernel vectors with squared norm at most 40.

**Likely death.** Good modular distance need not imply large integer Graver norm; affine codeword auxiliaries may absorb every attack.

## 5. Multi-prime arithmetic-transcript fingerprint

**Core trick.** Compute an assignment-dependent fingerprint through a sequential arithmetic circuit, e.g. \(h_i=h_{i-1}(1+(a_i-1)x_i)\bmod p\). Encode every multiplication gate by a constant truth-table selector and explicit Construction-A carries; combine several primes so a forged transcript must survive a CRT-scale test.

**Expected move.** Clause legality plus a globally consistent arithmetic transcript should make any signed forgery either alter many gates or pay a large terminal mismatch.

**Obstruction check.** O1: **not safely outside**—carry variables could exactly annihilate residuals, so they need heavy anchors. O2: one global transcript replaces private overlap. O3: all gates and carries are emitted in the lattice. O4: the fingerprint is nonlinear before linearization, but its table encoding may regain signed kernels. O5: enlarged transcripts lie outside raw linear hashes; affine combinations of honest transcripts remain a direct attack. O6: dropping one clause should disrupt its linked transcript segment, but may remain local. O7: no pair bags. O8: \(O(n)\) gates, no exponential node; linearized affine lifting persists.

**Falsification.** A zero-residual signed gate transcript with constant anchor excess, or a carry-only bypass.

**Smallest experiment.** Encode the four-variable obstruction over primes 5 and 7, enumerate gate selectors/carries in the exact anchor shell, and compare against a satisfiable control.

**Likely death.** Local gate-table relaxations transmit affine pseudodistributions exactly, while carries recreate G1.

## 6. Cosystolic occurrence complex

**Core trick.** Place variable occurrences on vertices/edges of an explicit bounded-degree 2- or 3-dimensional complex; encode consistency as coboundaries and clause legality as face constraints. A direct cosystolic-expansion proof would force every nontrivial inconsistency cochain to occupy \(\Omega(N)\) faces, producing Euclidean energy without a linear hierarchy.

**Expected move.** Turn a local contradiction into a macroscopic boundary, with polynomial gap after an explicit sequence of covers.

**Obstruction check.** O1: no slack. O2: consistency is globally topological rather than private-row. O3: every boundary map is an emitted integer matrix. O4: signed kernels become cocycles; nontrivial short cocycles are an explicit danger. O5: affine pseudodistributions may themselves be cocycles, so this is **not outside** the deepest obstruction. O6: expansion should charge a clause drop by its boundary. O7: higher-dimensional incidence is not pair-bag propagation. O8: constant-size faces give polynomial dimension and no root assignment table, but all boundary equations remain affine.

**Falsification.** A bounded-support cocycle carries the G7/G13 attack, or a cover merely replicates one violated clause without increasing its relative cost.

**Smallest experiment.** Build 2-, 3-, and 4-lifts of the nine-clause occurrence complex; use MILP to minimize anchored support subject to zero coboundary and harmful clause projection.

**Likely death.** Ordinary covers do not amplify sparse unsatisfaction; obtaining the required robust expansion may amount to rebuilding a PCP.
