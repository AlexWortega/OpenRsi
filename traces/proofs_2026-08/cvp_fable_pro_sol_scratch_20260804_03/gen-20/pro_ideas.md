Below, “anchor excess” means excess over the universal \(\|2z-1\|^2\) baseline. Each proposal uses only emitted lattice coordinates—no external feasibility filters.

1. **Bounded-additive-energy graph fingerprints**

**Mechanism.** Lift each local label to a graph point \((u,\phi(u))\), where the tags form a \(B_h\)-type set: no two signed combinations of at most \(h\) tags agree unless the underlying multisets agree. Apply linear coding only after this nonlinear graph lift. This uses the additive-uniqueness idea of Bose–Chowla (1962).

**Expected move.** Any zero-residual harmful selector must have negative mass \(\Omega(h)\), yielding \(\Omega(h)\) anchor excess.

**Obstruction audit.** **Slack-zeroing:** no slack. **Overlap failure:** tags cover overlapping label tuples. **External-filter gate:** graph membership gets explicit one-hot rows. **Radix/exact signed kernels:** precisely the relations \(B_h\) forbids, up to \(h\). **Parity/unique triple:** full tags, not bounded moments. **Clause drop:** normalization tags detect missing mass. **Honest-affine-span/hierarchy lift:** raw affine collisions need not lift through \(\phi\). **Barrington splice:** no flow representation. **Fixed pair-bag/no growth:** growth requires \(h=h(n)\), not yet established.

**Falsification.** A constant-support lifted affine collision.

**Smallest experiment.** Over \(\mathbb F_{257}^2\), search tags for the 16 global assignments with no signed affine relation of \(\ell_1\le8\); attach them to the nine-clause selectors and exactly search through excess 32.

**Likely death.** Field size or lift dimension becomes exponential when \(h=n^\delta\).

2. **Explicit carry-state towers rather than radix rows**

**Mechanism.** Represent every consistency quantity simultaneously in bases \(2,3,5\), with one-hot variables for each digit/carry transition. Unlike Generation 7, carries are lattice variables constrained by complete transition tables, not linear radix evaluations of an already-defeated residual.

**Expected move.** A signed alteration that preserves the low digit must propagate a non-Boolean carry through \(L\) levels, costing \(\Omega(L)\) anchor excess.

**Obstruction audit.** **Slack-zeroing:** no free slack; every carry has a table state. **Overlap failure:** occurrence values share entire towers. **External-filter gate:** all transitions are rows. **Radix/exact signed kernels:** outside G7’s “same residual, extra radix rows” assumption, but signed carry cycles remain possible. **Parity/unique triple:** carries encode complete labels, not moments. **Clause drop:** each level has normalization. **Affine-span/hierarchy lift:** raw affine combinations do not automatically satisfy transition-table lifts. **Barrington splice:** no path flow, though telescoping carry splices are analogous. **Fixed pair-bag/no growth:** \(L=\Theta(\log n)\) would give a candidate growth law.

**Falsification.** Any zero-residual carry cycle with \(O(1)\) negative states.

**Smallest experiment.** Four carry levels in bases \(2,3,5\) on the nine-clause instance; exact DP through anchor excess 32, explicitly seeding G7 and G11 witnesses.

**Likely death.** A two-level telescoping signed cycle repeats independently of \(L\).

3. **Sheaf consistency on a high-dimensional complex**

**Mechanism.** Put assignment tables on faces of an explicit simplicial complex and restriction maps on incidences. Seek an elementary integral cosystolic inequality: every normalized section is either a genuine global assignment or has many inconsistent incidences. This borrows topology, but would require a direct lattice proof rather than a PCP theorem.

**Expected move.** Unsatisfiability excludes genuine sections, while cosystolic expansion forces \(\Omega(N^\delta)\) nonzero residuals or comparable negative mass.

**Obstruction audit.** **Slack-zeroing:** no slack. **Overlap failure:** overlaps are coboundary coordinates globally. **External-filter gate:** stalk and restriction equations are emitted. **Radix/exact signed kernels:** exact kernels become integral global sections or cocycles. **Parity/unique triple:** higher faces expose local parity classes. **Clause drop:** normalized links give many boundary defects. **Honest-affine-span/hierarchy lift:** not outside automatically—affine pseudosections may be cocycles; this is the main test. **Barrington splice:** no temporal flow. **Fixed pair-bag/no growth:** expansion, rather than bag completeness, supplies the proposed scaling theorem.

**Falsification.** A bounded-support integral cocycle not generated by an assignment.

**Smallest experiment.** Build the complete \(3\)-dimensional restriction complex on the four-variable obstruction; compute integer kernels by SNF and minimize cocycle support/anchor excess by MILP.

**Likely death.** Nonlinear SAT fibers linearize into abundant exact pseudosections despite excellent topological expansion.

4. **Segre–Plücker rank-one rigidity**

**Mechanism.** Encode a global assignment as a decomposable tensor
\[
(1,x_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes(1,x_n).
\]
Enforce vanishing flattening minors using explicit multiplication-table selectors, then replicate carefully chosen minors through sparse tensor flattenings. Clause legality is read from tensor marginals.

**Expected move.** Exact zero residual should imply an integral rank-one tensor, hence globally consistent factors; otherwise many independent minors become nonzero.

**Obstruction audit.** **Slack-zeroing:** no slack. **Overlap failure:** one global tensor supplies all marginals. **External-filter gate:** multiplication and minor values are emitted tables. **Radix/exact signed kernels:** signed selectors must fake determinantal equations, not merely linear checks. **Parity/unique triple:** all relevant tensor degrees are available. **Clause drop:** the tensor’s constant coordinate fixes normalization globally. **Honest-affine-span/hierarchy lift:** the Segre lift is nonlinear, so the raw affine collision need not lift. **Barrington splice:** no flows. **Fixed pair-bag/no growth:** sparse flattening expansion is the missing scaling lemma.

**Falsification.** A constant-excess signed table satisfying every lifted minor but not projecting to rank one.

**Smallest experiment.** Use the \(2^4\) tensor for the nine-clause obstruction, all \(2\times2\) flattening minors, and exact shell search through excess 32.

**Likely death.** Linearized multiplication tables admit low-support “fake Plücker” cycles even when the actual minors are rigid.

5. **High-girth matroid/Graver encoding**

**Mechanism.** Make honest encodings equal-radius bases of a represented matroid, with clause and consistency violations corresponding to kernel circuits. Search for a representation whose harmful circuits have polynomial girth, then use circuit decomposition to lower-bound every integral kernel vector. This draws on Graver-basis methods (Graver, 1975).

**Expected move.** Every exact signed cheat contains a circuit of support \(\Omega(N^\delta)\), hence incurs that much anchor excess.

**Obstruction audit.** **Slack-zeroing:** no slack columns. **Overlap failure:** a single global representation replaces private clause matrices. **External-filter gate:** basis/syndrome coordinates are emitted. **Radix/exact signed kernels:** these are exactly the circuits being lengthened. **Parity/unique triple:** parity is harmless only if it remains a short circuit. **Clause drop:** deletion changes the global syndrome. **Honest-affine-span/hierarchy lift:** not automatically escaped; equal-syndrome honest encodings would reproduce G13, so honest bases must instead have equal distance but distinct syndromes. **Barrington splice:** no flow. **Fixed pair-bag/no growth:** matroid girth is the proposed asymptotic invariant.

**Falsification.** A bounded Graver element preserving the target-distance shell.

**Smallest experiment.** SAT-search a binary \(8\times24\) representation for the two-overlap clause core, then enumerate all circuits and exact nearest vectors through excess 16.

**Likely death.** Regular/representable matroid structure may be too tractable to encode arbitrary 3SAT while preserving large girth.

6. **Tensor-power amplification of a finite geometric gap**

**Mechanism.** Homogenize a finite survivor such as the complete pair-bag gadget, then form a Kronecker/tensor product whose honest vectors are pure tensors. Attempt to prove that distance multiplies under the product, turning a fixed ratio \(1+\varepsilon\) into \((1+\varepsilon)^{k/2}\) for \(k=\Theta(\log n)\).

**Expected move.** Convert the Generation-14 finite shell separation into a polynomial approximation factor without first finding a larger one-copy gap.

**Obstruction audit.** **Slack-zeroing:** inherited seed has no slack. **Overlap failure:** handled inside each pair-bag seed. **External-filter gate:** tensor basis and target are explicit. **Radix/exact signed kernels:** tensor products may create new entangled kernels, so this obstruction is not escaped. **Parity/unique triple:** seed charges audited examples. **Clause drop:** seed charges audited drops. **Honest-affine-span/hierarchy lift:** affine collisions may tensorize and are a direct threat. **Barrington splice:** no flow, but entangled tensors play the analogous role. **Fixed pair-bag/no growth:** this directly targets that obstruction via a multiplicativity lemma.

**Falsification.** A tensor-square vector cheaper than the product of one-copy minima.

**Smallest experiment.** Tensor-square a reduced three-variable all-eight-clauses pair-bag seed; run bounded Fincke–Pohst or shell DP and compare exact minima with the predicted products.

**Likely death.** Unrestricted integer vectors are entangled; CVP distance generally need not tensor-multiply. Dimension may also become quasipolynomial.

7. **Treewidth-or-wall structural routing**

**Mechanism.** Split by incidence-graph treewidth. If treewidth is \(O(\log n)\), solve 3SAT by dynamic programming and emit a fixed yes/no CVP instance; otherwise find a large wall/grid minor and route redundant assignment-consistency checks along many disjoint paths. The hoped-for lemma is that any signed inconsistency crossing the wall creates many defects.

**Expected move.** Obtain a polynomial gap from wall size on the high-treewidth branch, while disposing of locally structured formulas exactly. This uses the classical Robertson–Seymour graph-minor framework.

**Obstruction audit.** **Slack-zeroing:** no slack. **Overlap failure:** consistency is multiply routed globally. **External-filter gate:** every path check is emitted. **Radix/exact signed kernels:** wall cycles may still support exact circulations; not outside this obstruction. **Parity/unique triple:** redundant routes revisit every occurrence. **Clause drop:** dropping a routed clause breaks many paths. **Honest-affine-span/hierarchy lift:** affine pseudodistributions may traverse all routes exactly, so this must be tested. **Barrington splice:** signed circulation is the direct analogue. **Fixed pair-bag/no growth:** wall width supplies the candidate growth parameter.

**Falsification.** A constant-support signed circulation on arbitrarily large walls.

**Smallest experiment.** Construct \(3\times3\) and \(4\times4\) wall-routed copies of the two-clause overlap core; enumerate shortest integral kernel circuits and test whether their support grows with wall width.

**Likely death.** Large treewidth concerns graph connectivity, not semantic unsatisfiability; affine cheats may route through walls at constant cost.
