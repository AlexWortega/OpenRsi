1. **Iterated Lawrence lifting of the harmful kernel**  
**Mechanism.** Apply binary Lawrence liftings to the complete selector/check matrix, not to the formula copies separately. Such liftings preserve honest integral witnesses while potentially forcing every harmful kernel element to replicate across many blocks; the relevant invariant is Graver norm growth (Sturmfels, *Gröbner Bases and Convex Polytopes*, 1996).  
**Expected move.** Find a lift with harmful-distance growth \(\lambda>\mu\), then take \(O(\log n)\) levels and scale residual rows polynomially.  
**Obstruction audit.** G1: no slack. G2/3/5: global, not fixed-marginal/private. G6: all rows emitted; audit odd moduli. G7/9/11/13: their signed/parity vectors are candidate Graver elements—not outside until proven long. G12: drops replicate through lifts. G14/15: no pair bags or marginal tree. G19: no flow. G28/30: neither frozen min-plus composition nor literal tensor; nevertheless test isometries. G31/32: coupling is through lifted kernel equations, not additive moments. G33: ordinary anchors remain cospherical.  
**Falsifier/death.** A bounded-support lifted parity, or \(\lambda\le\mu\), kills it.  
**Experiment.** Lawrence-lift the 72-column G31 matrix once and twice; use exact DP/SNF to compute the shortest harmful zero-residual vector and matched-control radius.

2. **Delaunay-hole state confinement**  
**Mechanism.** Replace one-hot selectors by vertices on the nearest shell of a rational Delaunay polytope: legal states are all equally close to one target, while signed mixtures must land on a later lattice shell. Seek a polynomial family whose second-shell radius grows faster than its legal covering radius (cf. Conway–Sloane, *Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups*, 1999).  
**Expected move.** Obtain geometric \(0/1\) confinement before adding consistency checks, eliminating the need to punish negative coefficients indirectly.  
**Obstruction audit.** G1: no slack. G2/3/5: confinement is geometric and survives only if fiber products of holes compose. G6: the lattice and target are explicit. G7/9/11/13: not linear syndromes, but an affine collision may still produce another near lattice point—so not automatically outside. G12: zero-state/drop must lie beyond shell two. G14/15: no bag hierarchy. G19: no flow. G28/30: no fixed tile or tensor seed. G31/32: shell separation replaces additive Gram energy. G33: cosphericity is built into the Delaunay definition.  
**Falsifier/death.** Minkowski geometry may force a nearby unintended shell or only constant shell ratio.  
**Experiment.** Enumerate integral Gram matrices in dimensions \(3\!-\!6\), rational centers, and lattice points in \([-2,2]^d\), requiring seven OR labels on shell one; then glue two overlapping gadgets and enumerate shell two exactly.

3. **Torsion-valued clause defects**  
**Mechanism.** Map lifted clause states to 1-chains in an explicit finite cell complex whose first homology contains \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\). Honest satisfying encodings produce boundaries, whereas a violated clause should represent a fixed torsion generator; cosystolic expansion would make every integral filling large (basic framework: Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, 2002).  
**Expected move.** Choose \(q=n^\alpha\), making an unsatisfied affine class require either a nonzero scaled residual or a large chain.  
**Obstruction audit.** G1: no slack. G2/3/5: the invariant is global homology, not private rows. G6: boundary maps and targets are emitted; SNF verifies torsion. G7/9/11/13: outside only if the chain lift is nonlinear in raw selectors; a linear lift inherits the affine collision. G12: a drop has nonzero boundary. G14/15: not marginal propagation. G19: no accepting flow. G28/30: no tile/tensor rule. G31/32: parity blocks should add in homology rather than cancel metrically. G33: chain-coordinate anchors give equal completeness.  
**Falsifier/death.** A cheap signed 2-chain fills the alleged generator, or every defect map annihilating honest differences also annihilates G13.  
**Experiment.** Attach a \(q=5\) torsion chain complex to the nine-clause selectors, compute exact SNF, and enumerate all vectors through G31 cost 216.

4. **Noncommutative interval-product certification**  
**Mechanism.** Compile a short branching program but replace edge flow by a segment tree of full \((\text{start},\text{end},g)\) product selectors. Parent rows enforce group multiplication of child products; additional irreducible-representation blocks record matrix coefficients with equal Frobenius norm (Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*, 1977).  
**Expected move.** Prevent signed path splicing because every interval, not merely every layer, must possess a coherent noncommutative product, then polynomially weight root rejection.  
**Obstruction audit.** G1: no slack. G2/3/5: all scales share product states. G6: every convolution row is internal. G7/9/11/13: nonlinear product lookup enlarges the encoding, but signed pseudodistributions may still lift exactly. G12: an empty interval violates normalization. G14: not clause-pair bags. G15: unlike assignment marginals, nodes carry ordered products; nevertheless its affine-lift attack remains a direct test. G19: specifically replaces edge-flow conservation. G28/30: no frozen transfer table or tensor. G31/32: shared products are nonadditive. G33: unitary representation blocks are automatically equal-norm.  
**Falsifier/death.** A signed convolution pseudodistribution threads the entire tree.  
**Experiment.** Build a six-layer \(S_3\) program with one forbidden input; enumerate exact accepting fibers for edge flow, interval products, and interval products plus character blocks.

5. **Tanner-product rigidity for integral pseudocodewords**  
**Mechanism.** Place clause occurrences on an explicit bipartite expander and give every check a full local assignment table from a small linear code. Product-code checks are intended to make every zero-residual *integral* pseudocodeword globally honest, after which residual rows may safely receive weight \(n^\alpha\) (Sipser–Spielman, IEEE TIT 1996); this is a direct lattice construction, not an invocation of a PCP theorem.  
**Expected move.** Prove an integer-fiber rigidity lemma stronger than ordinary code distance: every non-honest vector violates \(\Omega(N)\) checks or pays large coefficient norm.  
**Obstruction audit.** G1: no slack. G2/3/5: expansion replaces private overlap. G6: checks are emitted. G7/9/11/13: exact pseudocodewords are the central unresolved threat, so not outside yet. G12: replicated normalization detects drops. G14: bounded-degree Tanner checks replace the complete fixed pair mesh. G15: not laminar. G19: no paths. G28/30: not frozen min-plus/tensor composition. G31/32: code distance seeks nonadditive defect spreading. G33: constant-weight local codewords give equal completeness.  
**Falsifier/death.** Expander codes may have short integral pseudocodewords despite excellent binary distance.  
**Experiment.** Take a 3-lift of the nine-clause incidence graph, emit all local tables, and use exact MILP/DP to compare shortest zero-residual pseudocodewords over lift sizes one, two, and three.

6. **Splitter-isolated Veronese fingerprints**  
**Mechanism.** Use an explicit perfect-hash/splitter family so every small support of clause coordinates is injective in some bucket. In each bucket encode quadratic or cubic lookup tags and then apply a constant-weight algebraic code; thus G13’s raw affine identity need not survive the enlarged Veronese image (Naor–Schulman–Srinivasan, FOCS 1995).  
**Expected move.** Detect every signed attack up to support \(s=n^\alpha\); any undetected attack must be large, while detected attacks incur polynomially scaled code distance.  
**Obstruction audit.** G1: no slack. G2/3/5: hashes cross arbitrary overlaps. G6: all bucket variables/checks are emitted. G7/9/11: higher lookup degree targets their kernels. G13: outside its raw-linear assumption, but lifted affine pseudodistributions remain possible. G12: every coordinate is isolated by some hash. G14/15: neither complete pair bags nor one fixed hierarchy. G19: no flow. G28/30: no seed recursion/tensor. G31/32: fingerprints couple selected supports rather than summing copy moments. G33: choose constant-weight codewords and certify the center exactly.  
**Falsifier/death.** The G13 affine combination may extend consistently to every bucket, or the splitter size may become superpolynomial.  
**Experiment.** Generate splitters for all supports of size at most seven among 72 selectors; emit quadratic bucket tables and test G7, G11, G13, DROP, and exhaustive cost-\(\le216\) states.

7. **Multi-prime Teichmüller valuation barrier**  
**Mechanism.** Give pair-bag labels nonlinear lookup tags modeled on Teichmüller representatives modulo several odd prime powers, then encode each resulting defect in balanced redundant digits with every carry equation included. Different shifted \(p\)-adic valuations should ensure that a nonzero bounded defect is expensive in at least one prime block (Serre, *Local Fields*, 1979).  
**Expected move.** First eliminate exact nonlinear-tag kernels; then choose prime powers of polynomial magnitude to obtain an \(n^c\) residual penalty without free carries.  
**Obstruction audit.** G1: no free slack. G2/3/5: tags span overlaps. G6: carries and modular rows are internal; odd primes avoid the recorded mod-2 bypass. G7: unlike linear radix rows, valuations act on nonlinear pair tags, but any exact tag kernel still kills it. G9/11/13: raw affine parity need not preserve Teichmüller pair tags; this requires audit, not assumption. G12: drops violate digit normalization. G14/15: only sparse selected pairs, no full mesh/tree. G19: no flow. G28/30: no min-plus/tensor seed. G31/32: prime blocks need not add independently. G33: balanced digit codewords must pass exact cosphericity first.  
**Falsifier/death.** A signed measure matches every pair tag, or carry variables restore a zero-cost representation.  
**Experiment.** Use primes \(3,5\), precision two, and the nine-clause instance; exhaustively compare raw parity, compatible double parity, DROP, and all vectors through squared cost 216.
