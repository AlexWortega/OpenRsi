# 1. Odd-group orbit-fold lemma

## Correct, with one missing standing hypothesis

The displayed lemma is valid provided \(D\) has at least one pointed word, so that \(\delta_*(D)<\infty\). Explicitly assume that the star-coordinate functional \(D\to\mathbb F_2\) is nonzero.

For \(P=\{*\}\), the proof is correct:

\[
S=\sum_{g\in G}gW
\]

has corner \(1\), is constant on every nontrivial orbit, and its value there is the orbit parity. Hence

\[
|S|=1+\ell\bigl(|F(W)|-1\bigr).
\]

The mixed-word quantifier is fully justified: no decomposability assumption on \(W\) is used.

The equality statement is also correct. If \(x\) is an invariant pointed minimum word, every noncorner supported orbit of \(x\otimes x\) has size \(\ell\), so

\[
|F(x\otimes x)|=1+\frac{\delta^2-1}{\ell}.
\]

Invariance also implies \(\ell\mid \delta-1\), and hence \(\ell\mid \delta^2-1\), so there is no hidden rounding issue.

## Missing extension needed in the residual-lineage proof

At later residual levels there are multiple fixed coordinates \(P_i\), so the lemma as stated does **not literally apply**, because the action is no longer free on \(X_i\setminus\{*\}\).

The required extension is nevertheless true:

> **Fixed-set orbit-fold lemma.** Suppose \(G\) fixes \(P\) pointwise, acts freely on \(X\setminus P\), and \(*\in P\). Then
> \[
> \delta_*(F(D\otimes D))
> \ge 1+\left\lceil\frac{\delta_*(D)^2-1}{|G|}\right\rceil.
> \]

Indeed, if \(r\) is the number of supported fixed pair-coordinates in the symmetrized word \(S\), then \(r\ge1\) and

\[
|S|=r+|G|(|F(W)|-r).
\]

Therefore

\[
|F(W)|
=\frac{|S|+(|G|-1)r}{|G|}
\ge 1+\frac{|S|-1}{|G|}
\ge 1+\frac{\delta_*(D)^2-1}{|G|}.
\]

The residual proof must cite this generalized lemma, not (OF) verbatim.

**Severity:** missing lemma/scope, not a failure of the lower bound.

# 2. Reduced pointed tensor and reduced orbit fold

## Exact distance \(1+d^2\): correct

Assume \(D\) has a pointed word, and define

\[
d=\min\{|u|:(1,u)\in D\}.
\]

The slicing argument is valid. If

\[
(1,M)=\sum_r(\alpha_r\beta_r,u_r\otimes v_r),
\]

then

\[
u=\sum_r\beta_r u_r
\]

satisfies \((1,u)\in D\), so at least \(d\) indices \(i\) have \(u_i=1\). For each such \(i\),

\[
\left(1,\sum_r (u_r)_i v_r\right)
=\sum_r (u_r)_i(\beta_r,v_r)\in D.
\]

Thus row \(i\) of \(M\) has at least \(d\) nonzeros. The selected rows are disjoint, giving \(|M|\ge d^2\). A pure tensor from a pointed minimum word gives equality.

Hence

\[
\delta_*(R(D))=1+d^2
\]

is correct for all mixed words.

The case \(d=0\) is also consistent: it means \((1,0)\in D\), and \(R(D)\) contains the corner-only word.

## Free-orbit reduced fold: correct

If an odd group acts freely on moving coordinates and preserves \(D\), the induced diagonal action on moving pairs is free. Symmetrization yields

\[
\delta_*(F_R(R(D)))
\ge 1+\left\lceil\frac{d^2}{|G|}\right\rceil.
\]

If \((1,u)\) is both invariant and moving-minimal, then \(|u|\) is divisible by \(|G|\), and the folded pure tensor has weight

\[
1+\frac{d^2}{|G|}.
\]

So the equality statement is correct.

## Wording correction

“100 arbitrary reduced tensor codes” should be replaced by “100 deterministic randomly generated small codes.” The script does not enumerate all small codes or all code structures.

**Severity:** wording only.

# 3. Residual-lineage theorem, rounding, and \(Q\)

## Fatal false quantifier

The theorem is false as written because it allows \(Q=1\). The proof says

\[
(Q-1)/g_0
\]

is a positive integer, but only divisibility was established.

For example, take an invariant pointed YES word supported only at \(*\), so

\[
a_0=s_0=Q=1.
\]

Then the first recurrence gives \(s_1=1\), contradicting \(s_1\ge3\).

The theorem must assume

\[
a_0-s_0=Q-1>0.
\]

Since \(g_0\ge3\) and \(g_0\mid Q-1\), this implies \(Q\ge4\).

A precise corrected opening is:

> Start with \(a_0=Q\), \(s_0=1\), \(b_0=Q+1\), and assume the propagated YES word has nonempty moving support. Equivalently, \(Q>1\); under freeness and invariance this forces \(Q-1\ge g_0\), hence \(Q\ge4\).

With this correction, the proof of \(s_1\ge3\) is valid.

## The recurrences are otherwise correct

For an invariant YES word,

\[
a_{i+1}=s_i^2+\frac{a_i^2-s_i^2}{g_i}
\]

is exact.

Under the stated lineage condition, the fixed support at the next level contains:

- \(s_i^2\) fixed-fixed classes;
- \(s_i(a_i-s_i)/g_i\) fixed-moving classes;
- the same number of moving-fixed classes.

Thus

\[
s_{i+1}\ge
s_i^2+\frac{2s_i(a_i-s_i)}{g_i}
\]

is correct.

The NO recurrence is valid after invoking the fixed-set extension of the orbit-fold lemma:

\[
b_{i+1}
=1+\left\lceil\frac{b_i^2-1}{g_i}\right\rceil.
\]

## Rounding calculation is correct

Let \(Q-1=g_0t\), \(t\ge1\). Then

\[
a_1=1+t(Q+1).
\]

Also,

\[
\frac{2Q+1}{g_0}=2t+\frac3{g_0},
\]

and since \(g_0\ge3\),

\[
\left\lceil\frac{2Q+1}{g_0}\right\rceil=2t+1.
\]

Therefore

\[
b_1=a_1+2t+1.
\]

The estimate \(b_1/a_1\le1+3/Q\) is valid.

The inequality

\[
\frac{\phi_g(y)}{\phi_g(x)}\le(y/x)^2
\]

is also correct, and the “surplus greater than one” argument correctly absorbs the ceiling. The nonrecovery claim when \(b_i<a_i\) is valid because \(b_i\le a_i-1\) and the YES surplus exceeds \(1\).

## Overstated scope of the no-go

The heading

> “Residual-lineage iteration cannot give a fixed polynomial gap”

is broader than the theorem proved. The theorem treats exactly:

- the specified residual-lineage fixed-sector condition;
- a base certificate \(Q\) versus \(Q+1\);
- NO propagation using only the orbit-fold lower bound.

It does not rule out:

- a stronger initial base gap;
- additional soundness information not represented by \(b_i\);
- a fresh action remobilizing inherited sectors.

Replace the heading by something such as:

> “Residual-lineage propagation from a \(Q\) versus \(Q+1\) base certificate has vanishing certified exponent.”

This matters for 3DM because the parity of a solution gives a slightly stronger elementary fact:

\[
|x|\equiv q\pmod2.
\]

Indeed, summing all \(3q\) syndrome equations gives \(3|x|\equiv3q\pmod2\). Thus, if there is no weight-\(q\) solution, the next possible weight is at least \(q+2\), not \(q+1\). The stated \(q+1\) lower bound remains true but is deliberately weaker. Consequently, the theorem with \(b_0=Q+1\) does not by itself constitute a no-go for every stronger 3DM-derived base certificate.

## Final asymptotic wording

The conclusion \(N_k^{o(1)}\) requires \(Q\to\infty\). “\(Q\) grows polynomially” is ambiguous: a polynomial upper bound alone is insufficient. Use:

> If \(Q\to\infty\) with the input size—e.g. \(Q\ge n^\alpha\) for some \(\alpha>0\)—then the exponent \(3/(Q\ln3)\) is \(o(1)\).

**Severity:** missing \(Q>1\) is fatal to the theorem as quantified; the other issues are scope corrections.

# 4. Functorial residual action

## Correct after making pointedness and induction explicit

The graph representation is correct under:

1. the star functional on \(D\) is nonzero;
2. \((1,0)\notin D\).

The moving projection is then injective, so for \(C=\pi(D)\) there is a unique linear form \(\phi:C\to\mathbb F_2\) with

\[
D=\{(\phi(u),u):u\in C\}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
R(D)=
\{((\phi\otimes\phi)(M),M):M\in C\otimes C\}.
\]

For a free abelian coordinate action, the residual action

\[
h[u,v]=[u,hv]
\]

is well-defined because commutativity makes it compatible with diagonal equivalence. It is free: if

\[
[u,hv]=[u,v],
\]

then some \(g\) satisfies \(gu=u\) and \(gv=hv\); freeness gives \(g=1\), then \(h=1\).

It preserves the folded code because \(1\otimes h\) preserves \(C\otimes C\), preserves the corner functional when \(\phi(hv)=\phi(v)\), and commutes with diagonal folding.

## “Reused indefinitely” needs one extra sentence

To iterate, one must show each folded code again has no corner-only word. This follows if the initial moving distance \(d\ge1\), since

\[
\delta_*(F_R(R(D)))
\ge1+\left\lceil\frac{d^2}{|G|}\right\rceil\ge2.
\]

Thus \((1,0)\) is absent at the next level, so the graph description and residual-action argument can be repeated inductively.

Add this explicitly. Without the assumption that \(D\) is pointed, \((1,0)\notin D\) alone is not enough to discuss pointed distances.

**Severity:** missing hypothesis/induction sentence, not a mathematical failure.

# 5. Conserved-exponent accounting

## Algebraic identity: correct under stronger hypotheses

The identity

\[
\frac{\log(b'/d')}{\log(n'/d')}
=
\frac{\log(b/d)}{\log(n/d)}
\]

is correct if all three exact recurrences hold:

\[
n'=\frac{n^2}{g},\qquad
d'=\frac{d^2}{g},\qquad
b'=\frac{b^2}{g}.
\]

But the prose currently assumes only “an invariant moving-minimum word.” To get both distance recurrences, one needs invariant minimum words for **both** the YES and NO codes. Alternatively, \(b\) must be explicitly described as an idealized threshold assumed to attain equality.

Correct statement:

> If the YES and NO moving minimum words are both invariant and therefore both attain their orbit-fold lower bounds, then the exact recurrences above hold and the normalized ratio is conserved.

If ceilings are present, the displayed logarithmic identity is not exact. “Up to integer rounding” and an exact equality should not appear in the same assertion.

## This is not the usual polynomial approximation exponent

The denominator is \(\log(n/d)\), not \(\log n\). Thus the conserved quantity is an exponent normalized by coordinate overhead relative to the YES distance. It is not, without further argument, the standard rank exponent

\[
\frac{\log(b/d)}{\log n}.
\]

Replace

> “The polynomial approximation exponent … is conserved”

by

> “The exponent normalized by the overhead ratio \(n/d\) is conserved.”

Also assume \(n>d>0\), since otherwise \(\log(n/d)\) vanishes or is nonpositive.

The claim that this normalized exponent tends to zero is valid only under an explicit asymptotic such as

\[
\log(b/d)=o(\log(n/d)).
\]

For an additive constant gap with \(d\to\infty\), this holds because \(\log(b/d)=O(1/d)\). It also holds for fixed \(d\) if \(n/d\to\infty\).

## \(q\)-notation is inconsistent in the reduced section

For the reduced construction, \(d,b\) are moving distances. Starting directly from the weak exact-cover certificate gives moving ratio

\[
\frac{q+1}{q},
\]

or, using the parity strengthening, at least \((q+2)/q\).

The ratio

\[
\frac{q+2}{q+1}
\]

is the ratio after adding a corner to a hypothetical moving \(q\) versus \(q+1\) gap. It is not the base moving ratio used by reduced tensoring.

Thus the final paragraph must either:

- use \((q+1)/q\) in the reduced-moving notation; or
- explicitly say it has switched back to full pointed weights.

With reduced moving weights and the weak additive-one gap, the exact sentence should be

\[
\left(1+\frac1q\right)^{2^k},
\]

and obtaining \(N^c\) indeed requires

\[
2^k=\Omega(q\log N).
\]

**Severity:** substantive notation and interpretation error; the underlying logarithmic algebra is correct.

# 6. Biset fixed-class count

## Fatal as stated

The claim that the pointed balanced product has exactly

\[
1+R_U
\]

fixed quotient classes generally omits the opposite cross sector \([*,v]\).

In a standard pointed regular cyclic bitorsor model:

- \([u,*]\) contributes one fixed class for each moving right-\(G\) orbit of \(U\);
- \([*,v]\) contributes fixed classes whenever the right \(K\)-action stays within the left \(G\)-orbit of \(v\), as it does for regular cyclic bitorsors;
- \([*,*]\) is the common corner.

If \(V\) has \(R_V\) regular moving bitorsor components, the usual count is

\[
1+R_U+R_V,
\]

not \(1+R_U\). Moving-moving classes can carry a free residual action.

More generally, the stabilizer of \([*,v]\) is

\[
\{k\in K:vk\in Gv\},
\]

so no exact count is possible without hypotheses on the relation between the left \(G\)- and right \(K\)-actions on \(V\).

The attached source for `verify_pointed_biset_cross.py` is absent, so it cannot resolve whether the script intentionally omitted one orientation. The mathematical prose must be corrected independently.

**Severity:** fatal to the exact biset fixed-class claim.
