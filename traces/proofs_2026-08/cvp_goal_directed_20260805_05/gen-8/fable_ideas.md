Below are six deliberately nonconvergent mechanisms. None relies on the prohibited document.

### 1. \(\mathbb Z_4\)-Kerdock residue ports

**Core trick.** Build the coefficient lattice by Construction A from a small \(\mathbb Z_4\)-linear Kerdock code, and decode ports by the nonlinear Gray map. Choose equal-norm codewords for legal NAND/COPY states; bent-code correlation may separate every other residue class, while exact coset leaders certify unrestricted coefficients.

**Expected move.** Directly prove FRONTIER with \(D=16\) or \(32\), replacing the failed affine \(E_6\) projection by nonlinear residue ports.

**Falsification.** A signed combination with legal residue and cost \(\le\mu\), or any adverse class with \(\lambda/\mu<65/64\).

**Smallest experiment.** Enumerate the length-16 \(\mathbb Z_4\) code, all four-word NAND labelings and COPY pairs; compute exact coset leaders and depth-two transfer tables.

**Obstruction audit.** G1: no slack; G2/G3: coset-leader theorem covers all \(\mathbb Z^D\); G5: glue complete residues; G6: no filters; G7: residue-zero lifts remain Euclidean-charged; G9/G11/G13: not automatically escaped—test every parity/affine collision; G12: zero/drop residue is adverse; G14/G31/G38: no finite extrapolation; G15/G19: no hierarchy/flow; G28: require exact transfer growth; G30: no tensor; G32/G37: no additive copy metric; G33/G34: no exterior tags; GD1/GD2: no ordered-pair or group-ring multiplication.

**Likely death.** The \(\mathbb Z_4\)-linear span contains a low-norm affine collision invisible to Gray decoding.

### 2. Graph cut lattices with min-cut Voronoi certificates

**Core trick.** Use a lattice of Voronoi’s first kind generated by an obtuse superbase from a weighted graph. Its relevant Voronoi vectors are cuts, so arbitrary-point nearest-neighbor claims can be reduced to exact submodular minimization rather than shell enumeration; selected cuts encode NAND/COPY states.

**Expected move.** Produce a complete global outside-\(K\) certificate and tune edge weights so adverse cuts have growth \(65/64\) above legal cuts.

**Falsification.** Any unlabelled cut ties the legal radius, or a signed non-cut lattice point yields a cheaper transfer.

**Smallest experiment.** Enumerate integer weights \(1,\ldots,8\) on \(K_6\), subset-codebooks modulo complementation, and solve equal-radius/separation inequalities before computing depth two.

**Obstruction audit.** G1/G6: no slack or filtering; G2/G3: Selling/min-cut certificate is all-integer; G5: glue entire cut boundary; G7: kernel points remain cut-lattice points; G9/G11/G13: explicitly minimized, not presumed absent; G12: empty cut is adverse; G14/G31/G38: finite search only proposes a symbolic certificate; G15/G19: no hierarchy or flow; G28: fresh non-product transfer table; G30: no tensor; G32/G37: nonorthogonal shared-edge energy; G33/G34: no bivectors or Gram repair; GD1/GD2: no pair lift or convolution.

**Likely death.** Delaunay cut cells contain too many subset cuts, forcing a malformed state onto the legal shell.

### 3. Inverse Delaunay synthesis with an analytic tail bound

**Core trick.** Search simultaneously for an arbitrary nonseparable rational Gram matrix \(Q\), center \(h=Qc\), and small integral codebook \(K\). Distances are linear in \((Q,h)\); impose legal equalities, adverse \(65/64\) inequalities, strict diagonal dominance \(Q\succeq\varepsilon I\), and then use \(\varepsilon\) to prove every unenumerated integer point lies outside the shell.

**Expected move.** Prove FRONTIER directly without committing to \(D_4\), \(E_6\), product coordinates, or affine shell projections.

**Falsification.** Exact infeasibility, a rational separating dual certificate, or an interior point found after reconstruction.

**Smallest experiment.** For \(D=8\), enumerate \(K\subset\{-1,0,1\}^8\); solve rational LPs for NAND/COPY and exact depth-two minima.

**Obstruction audit.** G1/G6: every coordinate emitted and charged; G2/G3: eigenvalue tail bound plus finite exact core covers \(\mathbb Z^D\); G5: full ports glued; G7/G9/G11/G12/G13/G15/G19: inserted as explicit universal adverse constraints; G14/G31/G38: no extrapolation without the tail proof; G28: arbitrary transfer, not frozen pair tiles; G30: no tensor; G32/G37: unrestricted cross-coordinate \(Q\), not additive orbit weights; G33/G34: no exterior ansatz; GD1/GD2: no flow or group ring.

**Likely death.** Equal completeness and global emptiness make the rational inequality system infeasible before transfer growth is considered.

### 4. Discrete-convex no-go for Cartesian binary ports

**Core trick.** Try to refute a broad frontier subclass: whenever three primitive truth-pairs are emitted as affine lattice ports, equal-radius NAND configurations force a midpoint, parallelogram, or Hilbert-basis combination representing a false/malformed port no farther away. This would generalize the \(D_4\) midpoint and quadratic-cube identities without assuming a particular lattice.

**Expected move.** Either disprove FRONTIER under Cartesian affine ports, or justify amending the roadmap to require nonprimitive redundant ports.

**Falsification.** One rank-\(\le4\) oriented lattice configuration satisfying equal legal radius and strict separation from every affine semigroup intruder.

**Smallest experiment.** Enumerate primitive truth-pair configurations up to \(\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb Z)\) for \(d\le4\); compute Hilbert bases and rational Farkas certificates.

**Obstruction audit.** This claims no escape construction. G1/G5/G6 must be included as emitted-coordinate hypotheses; G2/G3 require a theorem over the entire integer semigroup. G7/G9/G11/G12/G13/G15/G19 are candidate witnesses, not excluded. G14/G31/G38 are only finite evidence. G28/G32/G37 motivate but do not imply the theorem. G30, G33, G34, GD1, and GD2 concern unrelated tensor, exterior, pair-flow, or group-ring families.

**Likely death.** Redundant or nonprimitive port embeddings evade every midpoint/parallelogram argument, leaving only another limited no-go theorem.

### 5. Periodic min-plus cycle growth

**Core trick.** Amend the one-step condition \(\lambda>\mu\): construct the exact finite transfer automaton on \(K\), assign excess cost relative to legal propagation, and require every adverse strongly connected component to have positive minimum cycle mean. Min-plus spectral theory then gives exponential growth after bounded burn-in even if some one-step adverse transitions are cheap.

**Expected move.** Replace FRONTIER’s pointwise \(65/64\) inequality by a certified \(p\)-step ratio, while preserving the recursive-shell conclusion.

**Falsification.** Any reachable adverse zero/negative-excess cycle, especially a repeated parity or DROP state.

**Smallest experiment.** Recompute the G28 20-state table to a proved coefficient bound, then run exact minimum-cycle-mean and \(p=2,3,4\) block tests.

**Obstruction audit.** G1/G6: all transition costs remain emitted; G2/G3: still needs a Voronoi outside-\(K\) theorem; G5: full states are glued; G7/G9/G11/G12/G13/G15/G19 become explicit automaton states; G14/G31/G38: cycle theorem, not finite-depth extrapolation; G28: directly re-audited rather than evaded; G30: no tensor; G32/G37: compatible parity copies become zero-excess cycles and therefore reject the candidate; G33/G34: no exterior metric; GD1/GD2: no pair lift or convolution.

**Likely death.** DROP or diagonal parity supplies an adverse self-loop with exactly legal mean cost.

### 6. Regular-matroid Delaunay tiles

**Core trick.** Encode legal configurations as bases of a small regular matroid, realized by a totally unimodular matrix, and use a zonotopal/Delaunay metric whose nearest integral points admit circuit decomposition. NAND and COPY are represented by designated basis classes; every malformed selector contains a signed circuit whose energy can potentially be lower-bounded exactly.

**Expected move.** Prove unrestricted coercivity from total unimodularity and circuit elimination, rather than from affine shell projection or bounded enumeration.

**Falsification.** A balanced signed circuit preserving all ports with energy at most the legal basis energy.

**Smallest experiment.** Enumerate rank-4 regular matroids on at most eight elements, all NAND basis colorings, and rational orbit weights; use Graver-basis enumeration for complete depth-two minima.

**Obstruction audit.** G1/G6: no auxiliary slack or filters; G2/G3: regular-matroid circuits generate the full integer kernel; G5: glue full boundary incidence; G7: zero kernels are precisely audited circuits; G9/G11/G13/G19: signed parity/flow attacks are circuit candidates, not assumed absent; G12: deletion/drop is adverse; G14/G31/G38: no shell extrapolation without the circuit theorem; G15: no hierarchy; G28: different coercive invariant; G30: no tensor; G32/G37: shared nonorthogonal circuit energy, though additive circuits may still kill it; G33/G34: no exterior tags; GD1/GD2: no ordered pairs or group rings.

**Likely death.** Circuit elimination itself constructs the cheap signed splice that the metric was meant to forbid.

Classical touchstones: Hammons–Kumar–Calderbank–Sloane–Solé on \(\mathbb Z_4\)-linear Kerdock codes (1994); Selling/Voronoi reduction for obtuse superbases; Karp’s minimum-mean-cycle method (1978); and standard total-unimodularity/Graver-basis theory.
