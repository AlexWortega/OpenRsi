## Conditional syndrome-to-CVP transfer

Let `H ∈ F_2^{r×N}`, let `t ∈ im H`, and choose `v ∈ {0,1}^N` with `Hv=t`. Define

\[
\Lambda_H=\{z\in\mathbb Z^N:Hz=0\pmod 2\}.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{dist}_2(v,\Lambda_H)^2
 =\min\{|e|:e\in\mathbb F_2^N,\ He=t\}.
\]

Indeed, for `z∈Λ_H`, the residue `e=(v-z) mod 2` has syndrome `t`, and every odd coordinate of `v-z` contributes at least one to squared Euclidean norm. Conversely, for binary `e` of syndrome `t`, `z=v-e` lies in `Λ_H` and has squared distance `|e|`.

After row reduction and a column permutation, write an independent-row form of `H` as `[I_s | A]`, where `s=rank H`. In those coordinates,

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}2I_s&-\widetilde A\\0&I_{N-s}\end{pmatrix}
\]

is an explicit integer basis of `Λ_H`: its columns lie in the kernel modulo two and its determinant has absolute value `2^s`, equal to the lattice index. Thus a Hamming approximation factor `γ` transfers to Euclidean factor `sqrt(γ)` at lattice rank `N`. No polynomial Hamming gap is supplied here.

## Exact-cover incidence baseline

For a 3-dimensional matching instance `U⊆T_1×T_2×T_3`, with `|T_i|=q`, let `A∈F_2^{3q×|U|}` be the element-triple incidence matrix and let `t` be the all-one syndrome. If `Ax=t`, then every element has a positive odd number of selected incident triples. Therefore the total selected incidence is at least `3q`. Every selected triple contributes exactly three, so `3|x|≥3q`. If `|x|≤q`, equality holds: every element has selected degree exactly one and `x` is a perfect matching. Conversely every perfect matching is a weight-`q` solution.

This gives exact NP-hardness but only a `q` versus `q+1` lower bound.

## Relative-quotient proposal and its affine-closure obstruction

A natural attempt is to let `C=ker A` and choose `B⊆C` containing all differences of perfect matchings. If all legal solutions lie in one affine class `x_0+B`, one may try to attach a code or chain complex with large relative distance

\[
d(C/B)=\min\{|z|:z\in C\setminus B\}.
\]

The elementary conditional bound is valid: if an illegal fiber point is `x=x_0+z` with `z∈C\setminus B`, then

\[
|x|\ge |z|-|x_0|\ge d(C/B)-|x_0|.
\]

However, the classifier premise has a basic affine-closure problem. Let `M` be the set of perfect-matching indicators in one instance and fix `x_0∈M`. Every linear space `B` containing all legal differences `x+x_0`, `x∈M`, also contains their span. Hence its legal affine class contains

\[
x_0+\operatorname{span}\{x+x_0:x\in M\},
\]

which is exactly the affine hull over `F_2` of all perfect matchings. In particular, every odd XOR of perfect matchings lies in this class. An odd XOR need not itself be a perfect matching: selected element degrees remain odd modulo two but may be three or larger as integers. Such a point is an illegal odd cover that **no subsequent linear quotient metric can distinguish from the legal class**.

The existence of such illegal affine combinations is not asserted for every instance. It occurs in the exact finite families checked by `experiments/verify_exchange_quotient.py`: among 120 deterministic small dictionaries, 32 have an illegal odd cover in the minimal affine hull of their perfect matchings. In 66, an illegal cover lies in the larger span generated by all explicit 2↔2 and 3↔3 packing exchanges. These are finite computational claims reproduced by exhaustive enumeration, not asymptotic theorems.

Therefore the proposed direct interface “identify every perfect matching, then amplify relative distance” is wounded. A surviving mutation must make the legal witness family affine-closed, canonical/unique, or separately protected; ordinary quotienting of all legal differences is insufficient.

## Odd-group orbit folding of pointed tensor squares

Let a finite group `G` of odd order `ell` act on the coordinate set `X`, fixing the distinguished coordinate `*` and acting freely on `X\{*}`. Let the pointed binary code `D≤F_2^X` be `G`-invariant. The diagonal action on `X×X` fixes `(*,*)` and is free everywhere else. Define the orbit-parity fold

\[
 F:\mathbb F_2^{X\times X}\longrightarrow
 \mathbb F_2^{(X\times X)/G},\qquad
 F(W)_O=\sum_{u\in O}W_u.
\]

### Lemma (mixed-word orbit-fold lower bound)

Writing `δ=δ_*(D)`,

\[
 \delta_*\bigl(F(D\otimes D)\bigr)
 \ge 1+\left\lceil\frac{\delta^2-1}{\ell}\right\rceil.
 \tag{OF}
\]

If `D` has a pointed minimum word `x` invariant under `G`, equality holds.

### Proof

Take any `W∈D⊗D` with `W_{*,*}=1`. Since `D⊗D` is invariant, its group symmetrization

\[
 S=\sum_{g\in G}gW
\]

also lies in `D⊗D`. Because `ell` is odd, `S_{*,*}=ell W_{*,*}=1` in `F_2`. On every nontrivial orbit `O`, `S` is constant and its common value is exactly the orbit parity `F(W)_O`. Therefore

\[
 |S|=1+\ell\bigl(|F(W)|-1\bigr).
\]

The full pointed tensor theorem gives `|S|≥δ²`, proving (OF). This argument applies to every mixed tensor word.

For the upper bound, let `x` be a `G`-invariant pointed word of weight `δ`. Its tensor square is invariant and its support outside `(*,*)` is a union of free orbits. Each supported orbit has odd cardinality `ell`, so its orbit parity is one. Hence

\[
 |F(x\otimes x)|=1+\frac{\delta^2-1}{\ell},
\]

which meets (OF). ∎

`experiments/verify_cyclic_tensor_fold.py` constructs the folded generator and exhaustively enumerates all image words. Across 120 deterministic `ell=3` examples—20 phase-replicated hostile/random pointed codes and 100 small naturally invariant cyclic codes—the theorem's lower bound holds. In 37 natural examples, folded distance is strictly below the full tensor value `δ²`, often exactly at the orbit-count scale. These computations only check the implementation; the lemma above is the proof.

This still does not yield polynomial-size amplification. The missing issue is now parameter/symmetry construction, not mixed-word soundness. To attain the matching upper bound, a YES minimum word must be invariant. The naive way to create such a word repeats every nonstar coordinate across `G`, changing length `L` to `1+ell(L-1)` and its weight `d` to `1+ell(d-1)`. One subsequent fold has length

\[
1+\frac{(1+\ell(L-1))^2-1}{\ell},
\]

which is at least of order `ell L²`: the symmetry preparation spends more than the fold saves. Iteration additionally requires a large residual free action after each quotient. A useful construction must obtain the symmetry intrinsically (for example from a lift/balanced product) without paying its whole order as fresh padding, preserve an invariant sparse YES word, and keep the generator explicit.

### Residual-lineage iteration cannot give a fixed polynomial gap

The most natural attempt to replenish symmetry uses the residual action after each diagonal quotient. The following theorem rules out that family under an explicit lineage hypothesis.

At level `i`, let an odd group `G_i`, of order `g_i≥3`, act on coordinates `X_i`, fix a set `P_i` containing `*`, and act freely off `P_i`. Let `D_i` be invariant and let

\[
D_{i+1}=F_i(D_i\otimes D_i).
\]

Assume every quotient orbit represented by a pair having at least one endpoint in `P_i` is fixed by the next action. This holds for the standard residual `(G_i×G_i)/diag(G_i)` action when `G_i` is abelian, and for its subgroups. Assume the propagated YES word is invariant. Write

* `a_i` for its weight;
* `s_i` for its supported weight inside `P_i`;
* `b_i` for the NO threshold propagated only by (OF).

Start with `a_0=Q`, `s_0=1`, and `b_0=Q+1`. Since invariant moving support is a union of free orbits, `g_i | (a_i-s_i)`. Exact orbit counting and (OF) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{i+1}&=s_i^2+\frac{a_i^2-s_i^2}{g_i},\\
s_{i+1}&\ge s_i^2+\frac{2s_i(a_i-s_i)}{g_i},\\
b_{i+1}&=1+\left\lceil\frac{b_i^2-1}{g_i}\right\rceil.
\end{aligned}
\tag{R}
\]

#### Theorem

For every `k≥1`,

\[
s_k\ge3^{2^{k-1}},\qquad N_k:=|X_k|\ge3^{2^{k-1}}.
\]

If the certified ratio `Γ_k=b_k/a_k` is at least one, then

\[
\Gamma_k
 \le \left(1+\frac3Q\right)^{2^{k-1}}
 \le N_k^{3/(Q\ln3)}.
\tag{RN}
\]

Consequently no fixed exponent `c>0` is certified once `Q>3/(c ln 3)`. If `N_k` is polynomial in an input size for which `Q` grows polynomially, the certified ratio is `N_k^{o(1)}`.

#### Proof

At the first step, `(a_0-s_0)/g_0=(Q-1)/g_0` is a positive integer, so (R) gives `s_1≥3`. Thereafter `s_{i+1}≥s_i²`, proving the fixed-support and length bounds.

For real `y≥x≥1`, put `φ_g(x)=1+(x²-1)/g`. Direct cross-multiplication gives

\[
\frac{\phi_g(y)}{\phi_g(x)}\le(y/x)^2.
\tag{1}
\]

For `i≥1`, `s_i≥3`, and the YES recurrence is

\[
a_{i+1}=\phi_{g_i}(a_i)+(s_i^2-1)(1-1/g_i),
\]

whose surplus is greater than one. Since `ceil(z)≤z+1`, if `b_i≥a_i`, (1) and this surplus imply

\[
\frac{b_{i+1}}{a_{i+1}}\le(b_i/a_i)^2.
\tag{2}
\]

If `b_i<a_i`, integer arithmetic in (R), together with `s_i≥3`, gives `b_{i+1}<a_{i+1}`, so a nontrivial certificate never recovers.

It remains to bound the first fold. Write `t=(Q-1)/g_0≥1`. Then

\[
a_1=1+t(Q+1).
\]

Moreover `(2Q+1)/g_0=2t+3/g_0`, so

\[
b_1=a_1+2t+1,
\qquad
\Gamma_1\le1+3/Q.
\]

Iterating (2) yields the first inequality in (RN). The elementary bound `1+x≤e^x` gives

\[
\Gamma_k\le\exp(3\cdot2^{k-1}/Q).
\]

Finally `N_k≥3^{2^{k-1}}`, i.e. `2^{k-1}≤ln N_k/ln3`, proves (RN). ∎

`experiments/verify_residual_fold_parameters.py` checks 14,773 exact recurrence transitions and the ratio inequalities. The theorem does **not** cover a genuinely new symmetry that remobilizes inherited cross sectors.

### Reduced pointed tensor removes the cross sectors without losing distance

There is a useful exact mutation. Split the coordinate space as `F_2 e_* ⊕ V` and write every pointed-code word as `(α,u)`. Define

\[
R(D)=\operatorname{span}\{(\alpha\beta,\ u\otimes v):
 (\alpha,u),(\beta,v)\in D\}
 \subseteq \mathbb F_2\oplus(V\otimes V).
\]

This is obtained from `D⊗D` by deleting both star-cross rectangles and retaining only `(*,*)` plus moving-moving coordinates.

Let

\[
d=\min\{|u|:(1,u)\in D\}=\delta_*(D)-1.
\]

Then

\[
\delta_*(R(D))=1+d^2.
\tag{RT}
\]

To prove the lower bound, write any pointed word of `R(D)` as

\[
(1,M)=\sum_r (\alpha_r\beta_r,u_r\otimes v_r).
\]

Apply the linear functionals on `D` that read the star coefficient. Contracting the original tensor expression in the second factor gives

\[
u=\sum_r\beta_r u_r
\]

with `(1,u)∈D`; hence at least `d` moving row indices of `M` have contracted bit one. For each such row `i`, the row vector of `M` is `\sum_r (u_r)_i v_r`, and together with star bit one it is a pointed word of `D`, so it has at least `d` moving nonzeros. The rows are disjoint, giving `|M|≥d²`. A pure tensor of a moving-minimum pointed word attains equality. This is the same slicing argument as full pointed tensor distance, but the compulsory corner contributes separately and no star-cross coordinate is needed.

If an odd group `G` acts freely on the moving coordinates and preserves `D`, diagonal orbit-parity folding of the moving-moving block gives, by the same symmetrization proof,

\[
\delta_*\bigl(F_R(R(D))\bigr)
 \ge1+\left\lceil\frac{d^2}{|G|}\right\rceil.
\tag{ROF}
\]

An invariant moving-minimum pointed word attains the orbit-count value. `experiments/verify_reduced_orbit_fold.py` exactly enumerates 100 arbitrary reduced tensor codes and 100 invariant folded codes, checking (RT) and (ROF) against all mixed image words.

This lemma removes the specific fixed star-cross sectors behind the residual-lineage theorem. It still does not provide a polynomial-gap iteration: one must construct fresh commuting actions on the moving sector, preserve an invariant sparse affine witness, and show the reduced/folded image can be represented as an explicit binary syndrome instance at every level with useful length recurrence. The biset experiment below addresses only the coordinate action.

Classical biset composition provides such a coordinate-level renewal mechanism. For an `(H,G)`-biset `U` and `(G,K)`-biset `V`, the balanced quotient `U×_G V` retains the fresh right `K`-action. However, in the pointed construction where both factors have a fixed star, every class `[u,*]` is fixed by `K`. For regular cyclic moving actions, if `U` has `R_U` moving regular `G`-orbits, these form exactly `1+R_U` fixed quotient classes (including `[*,*]`); all other classes can carry a free fresh action. `experiments/verify_pointed_biset_cross.py` checks this exact count on 54 small products. Thus bisets evade residual lineage but do not automatically remove the pointed cross-sector growth. No compatible code/affine-class distance theorem is proved here. Gauge-fixing or projecting those sectors would require a new soundness argument against mixed tensors.

### Explicit residual reduced-fold assembly and exponent accounting

The reduced product is functorial. If `(1,0)∉D`, let `C` be the moving projection of `D` and let `φ:C→F_2` be its star form, so `D={(φ(u),u):u∈C}`. Then

\[
R(D)=\{((\phi\otimes\phi)(M),M):M\in C\otimes C\}.
\]

If a free odd abelian action preserves `C` and `φ`, the residual action on diagonal moving-pair orbits, `h[u,v]=[u,hv]`, is free and preserves the folded code. Thus one group action can be reused indefinitely after cross-sector deletion.

`experiments/verify_reduced_fold_3dm.py` implements this end to end. For a small exact 3DM YES instance with moving distance 3 and a NO odd-cover instance with moving distance 5, one-time `Z_3` installation gives distances 9 and 15. Two reduced residual folds give exact mixed-word minima `(27,75)` and `(243,1875)`. The ratios are exactly `(5/3)^2` and `(5/3)^4`; moving lengths grow from 24 to 192 to 12288.

More generally, when an invariant moving-minimum word attains the orbit lower bound, one fold has idealized recurrences

\[
n'=n^2/g,\qquad d'=d^2/g,\qquad b'=b^2/g
\]

(up to integer rounding), where `d,b` are YES/NO moving distances. Therefore

\[
\frac{\log(b'/d')}{\log(n'/d')}
 =\frac{\log(b/d)}{\log(n/d)}.
\]

The polynomial approximation exponent relative to overhead above the YES baseline is conserved, not amplified. For an additive base gap and a dictionary whose spare-coordinate overhead grows, this exponent tends to zero. This accounting is distinct from the earlier fixed-cross theorem: reduced products remove fixed-cross growth, but do not improve the base exponent.

A possible escape is to use a non-invariant YES word, allowing group order larger than YES distance so orbit cancellations compress completeness more than naive invariant replication. Exact tests do not support the simplest version. In `verify_superbudget_3dm.py`, cyclic sheets each contain an independent pointed 3DM witness, so base minima remain 3 (YES) and 5 (NO) for `ell=3,5,7,11`. Every reduced orbit fold has exact distance 9 and 25 respectively—the full square, despite lower bounds as small as 1 and 3. Two broader cyclic searches likewise found no nontrivial `ell>d>1` example attaining the orbit floor. These are finite observations, not a no-go theorem.

For a base ratio `(q+2)/(q+1)`, after `k` ideal squarings the ratio is `(1+1/(q+1))^{2^k}`. Achieving a polynomial factor in final rank requires `2^k=Ω(q log N)` after accounting for the rank recurrence. Thus the number and size of folds cannot be omitted from the proof.

## Honest conclusion

No polynomial-gap binary NCP reduction has been obtained, so the conditional lattice transfer does not establish CVP hardness within any fixed polynomial factor. The exact-cover quotient is wounded by illegal affine combinations of legal witnesses. The cyclic fold has encouraging tiny exact data but lacks the central mixed-word theorem and viable asymptotic parameter recurrence.
