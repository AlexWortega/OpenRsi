### 1. **Composable bisets: quotient one symmetry while retaining a genuinely fresh commuting symmetry**

**Source.** Serge Bouc, *Biset Functors for Finite Groups*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1990, Springer, 2010; see also Bouc’s notes “Bisets and associated functors.” ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-11297-3?utm_source=openai))

**Exact construction.** Let \(U\) be an \((H,G)\)-biset and \(V\) a \((G,K)\)-biset. Thus \(U\) has commuting left \(H\)- and right \(G\)-actions, while \(V\) has commuting left \(G\)- and right \(K\)-actions. Define

\[
U\times_G V=(U\times V)/{\sim},\qquad
(ug,v)\sim (u,gv).
\]

Equivalently, quotient \(U\times V\) by

\[
(u,v)\cdot g=(ug,g^{-1}v).
\]

The quotient remains an \((H,K)\)-biset:

\[
h\,[u,v]\,k=[hu,vk].
\]

Composition is associative:

\[
(U\times_G V)\times_K W
\cong U\times_G(V\times_K W),\qquad
[[u,v],w]\longmapsto [u,[v,w]].
\]

If the anti-diagonal \(G\)-action is free, then

\[
|U\times_G V|=\frac{|U||V|}{|G|}.
\]

More generally, for the residual \(K\)-action one has the exact fixed-point formula

\[
\bigl|\operatorname{Fix}_{U\times_GV}(k)\bigr|
 =
 \frac1{|G|}
 \sum_{g\in G}
 \bigl|\{u:ug=u\}\bigr|\,
 \bigl|\{v:gvk=v\}\bigr|.
\tag{B1}
\]

In particular, if the right \(G\)-action on \(U\) is free,

\[
\bigl|\operatorname{Fix}_{U\times_GV}(k)\bigr|
 =
 \frac{|U|}{|G|}\,|\operatorname{Fix}_V(k)|.
\tag{B2}
\]

Hence a free right \(K\)-action on \(V\) remains free after quotienting by \(G\).

For a chain of bisets

\[
U_0\; {}_{G_0}\!-\!{}_{G_1},\quad
U_1\; {}_{G_1}\!-\!{}_{G_2},\quad\dots,\quad
U_t\; {}_{G_t}\!-\!{}_{G_{t+1}},
\]

with all middle quotient actions free,

\[
\left|U_0\times_{G_1}U_1\times_{G_2}\cdots\times_{G_t}U_t\right|
 =
 \frac{\prod_{i=0}^{t}|U_i|}
      {\prod_{i=1}^{t}|G_i|}.
\tag{B3}
\]

**Why this is the highest-leverage match.** This is exactly a classical mechanism for “spending” \(G_i\) in a diagonal quotient while retaining \(G_{i+1}\) as a fresh action on **all** quotient coordinates. The next action is not a subgroup of \((G_i\times G_i)/\operatorname{diag}G_i\), so it lies outside the residual-lineage hypothesis in the supplied no-go theorem. Formula (B2) proves that freeness is renewed rather than inherited only on old moving sectors.

**Distinguished coset and invariant word.** A distinguished coordinate can be an orbit \([u_*,v_*]\), or a distinguished coset in a transitive biset. A \(K\)-invariant binary word is a union of residual \(K\)-orbits. If the residual action is free, its moving weight is necessarily a multiple of \(|K|\). Thus the biset construction solves the **symmetry renewal** issue but does not itself solve the sparse-invariant-word issue.

**How to adapt/verify.**

1. Choose explicit permutation bisets \(U_i\) and enumerate the quotient tuples.
2. Verify (B1) directly for every \(k\).
3. Linearize to permutation modules \(\mathbb F_2[U_i]\).
4. Search for equivariant boundary maps and small \(G_{t+1}\)-invariant cycles supported on only a few residual orbits.
5. The decisive hostile experiment is whether cross sectors that were fixed under the old residual action are moved by the new right action.

### 2. **Lifted products over group algebras: quotient-sized chain complexes without expanding a full tensor**

**Source.** Pavel Panteleev and Gleb Kalachev, “Asymptotically Good Quantum and Locally Testable Classical LDPC Codes,” arXiv 2021; STOC 2022. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03654?utm_source=openai))

**Exact construction.** Put \(R=\mathbb F_qG\). Let \(A,B\) be free chain complexes over \(R\), with \(A\) a right \(G\)-complex and \(B\) a left \(G\)-complex. If \(\widetilde A_{G,i}\) and \(\widetilde B_{G,j}\) are bases over \(R\), then the ordinary \(\mathbb F_q\)-basis of \(A\otimes_G B\) is

\[
a\cdot g\cdot b,
\qquad
a\in\widetilde A_{G,i},\quad
g\in G,\quad
b\in\widetilde B_{G,j}.
\]

The boundary is

\[
\partial(a\cdot g\cdot b)
 =
 (\partial_Aa)\cdot g\cdot b
 +
 (-1)^i a\cdot g\cdot(\partial_Bb).
\tag{LP1}
\]

Consequently, if \(a_i=\operatorname{rank}_R A_i\) and \(b_j=\operatorname{rank}_R B_j\), then

\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(A\otimes_G B)_n
 =
 |G|\sum_{i+j=n}a_i b_j.
\tag{LP2}
\]

This should be compared with an unbalanced tensor of the expanded \(\mathbb F_q\)-complexes, whose corresponding scale is \(|G|^2\sum a_i b_j\).

For two-term complexes represented by matrices over \(R\), the lifted-product boundary matrices are the group-algebra Kronecker blocks

\[
M_1=
\begin{bmatrix}
\partial_A\otimes I & I\otimes\partial_B
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
M_2=
\begin{bmatrix}
I\otimes\partial_B\\
\partial_A\otimes I
\end{bmatrix},
\tag{LP3}
\]

with signs/transposes chosen according to the chain-complex convention. Expanding each \(R\)-entry via the regular representation gives the binary or \(q\)-ary matrix. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.09271))

**Distance theorem for mixed words.** Their main theorem constructs, for every field \(\mathbb F_q\) and every target rate \(R_0\in(0,1)\), explicit qLDPC families

\[
[[n,k,d]]_q,\qquad k>R_0n,\qquad d=\Theta(n).
\tag{LP4}
\]

The distance quantifies over every nontrivial homology representative, not merely pure tensors. Their product-expansion argument explicitly analyzes arbitrary local matrices/rows in a product code and is therefore relevant to the “mixed tensor word” requirement. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03654))

**Action and fixed points.** Translation on the middle coordinate,

\[
h:(a,g,b)\longmapsto(a,hg,b)
\]

is fixed-point-free for \(h\ne 1\), whenever it preserves the chosen equivariant matrices. With \(r_n=\sum_{i+j=n}a_i b_j\),

\[
|\operatorname{Fix}(h)|=
\begin{cases}
|G|r_n,&h=1,\\
0,&h\ne1.
\end{cases}
\]

Appending a fixed star changes this to \(1+|G|r_n\) coordinates and one fixed coordinate for every nonidentity \(h\).

**Why relevant.** This is the strongest known pre-2024 answer to the “code-dependent structured compression of a tensor” part: it retains one group coordinate rather than two, and its distance theorem covers arbitrary homological combinations.

**Limitation.** The theorem does not identify a pointed sparse \(G\)-invariant witness or prove an orbit-parity image theorem. The asymptotically good families use expansion rather than a distinguished affine coset.

**Verification path.** Implement matrices in \(\mathbb F_2G\) as sparse dictionaries \(G\to\mathbb F_2\), build (LP3), and compare exhaustive minimum distances with the fully expanded product for groups of orders \(3,5,7\). Then impose a candidate invariant cycle and test whether it survives a second, commuting biset action.

### 3. **Balanced products of chain complexes: the explicit diagonal quotient and residual-action interface**

**Source.** Nikolas P. Breuckmann and Jens N. Eberhardt, “Balanced Product Quantum Codes,” *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 67(10), 2021, 6653–6674. ([discovery.ucl.ac.uk](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10133213/?utm_source=openai))

**Exact coordinate construction.** Let \(H\) act on the right of bases \(X_i\) of \(C_i\) and on the left of bases \(Y_j\) of \(D_j\). The balanced coordinate set is

\[
X_i\times_HY_j
 =
 \{[x,y]\},
\qquad
[xh,y]=[x,hy].
\tag{BP1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
[x,y]=\{(xh,h^{-1}y):h\in H\}.
\]

The degree-\(n\) chain basis is

\[
(C\otimes_HD)_n
 =
 \bigoplus_{i+j=n}\mathbb F_2[X_i\times_HY_j],
\]

and the boundary is

\[
\partial[x,y]
 =
 \sum_{x'\in\partial x}[x',y]
 +
 \sum_{y'\in\partial y}[x,y'].
\tag{BP2}
\]

Algebraically,

\[
C\otimes_HD
 =
 C\otimes_{\mathbb F_2[H]}D.
\tag{BP3}
\]

If the anti-diagonal \(H\)-action is free, then

\[
|(X\times_HY)_n|
 =
 \frac1{|H|}
 \sum_{i+j=n}|X_i||Y_j|.
\tag{BP4}
\]

For odd \(|H|\), the paper proves the balanced Künneth formula

\[
H_n(C\otimes_HD)
 \cong
 \bigoplus_{i+j=n}H_i(C)\otimes_HH_j(D).
\tag{BP5}
\]

These formulas are explicit in the paper. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.09271))

**Concrete distance theorem.** For their cyclic balanced product \(C(X,L)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}C(C_\ell)\), Corollary 23 gives

\[
N=3|X_1|,
\]

\[
K\ge \left(\frac{2k_L}{s}-1\right)\frac{|X_1|}{\ell},
\]

\[
D_Z\ge |X_1|\min\{\alpha_{\rm ho}/2,\alpha_{\rm ho}\beta_{\rm ho}/4\},
\]

and

\[
D_X\ge
\min\left\{
\alpha_{\rm co}|X_1|,
\frac{\alpha_{\rm co}|X_1|}{2},
\frac{\ell\alpha_{\rm co}}{4s},
\frac{\ell\alpha_{\rm co}\beta_{\rm co}}{4s}
\right\}.
\tag{BP6}
\]

The distance is over all representatives in the relevant homology/cohomology classes. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.09271))

**Why relevant.** This supplies the exact linearization of the diagonal coordinate quotient in the proof. More importantly, combining it with the biset machinery in Find 1 gives the natural mutation

\[
C_0\otimes_{G_1}C_1\otimes_{G_2}\cdots\otimes_{G_t}C_t,
\]

where each factor is a bimodule complex. The next symmetry can be an independent commuting action, rather than residual lineage.

**Invariant word.** If \(z_i\) are invariant cycles and their external tensor survives the balancing relations, then

\[
[z_0\otimes\cdots\otimes z_t]
\]

is an invariant cycle. Its orbit support must be counted after quotienting; for free middle actions its pure-tensor support is

\[
\frac{\prod_i |\operatorname{supp}z_i|}
     {\prod_{i=1}^t|G_i|}
\]

provided the action on that support is free. Mixed cycles can be lighter, so a product-expansion or projection theorem remains necessary.

### 4. **Voltage and permutation lifts: a fresh deck action at every stage**

**Source.** Jonathan L. Gross and Thomas W. Tucker, “Generating All Graph Coverings by Permutation Voltage Assignments,” *Discrete Mathematics* 18, 1977; ordinary voltage constructions were developed further in their classical graph-covering work. ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0012365X77901315?utm_source=openai))

**Exact construction.** For a directed base graph \(B\) and a voltage assignment

\[
\alpha:D(B)\to\Gamma,\qquad
\alpha(e^{-1})=\alpha(e)^{-1},
\]

the derived graph has

\[
V(B^\alpha)=V(B)\times\Gamma
\]

and lifted dart

\[
(e,g):(u,g)\longrightarrow(v,g\alpha(e))
\quad\text{for }e:u\to v.
\tag{V1}
\]

Left translation

\[
h\cdot(v,g)=(v,hg)
\tag{V2}
\]

is a deck action. It is free:

\[
|\operatorname{Fix}(h)|=
\begin{cases}
|V(B)||\Gamma|,&h=1,\\
0,&h\ne1.
\end{cases}
\tag{V3}
\]

The quotient is the base graph. For permutation voltages \(\alpha(e)\in S_m\), replace the group coordinate by \(i\in[m]\) and use

\[
(e,i):(u,i)\longrightarrow(v,\alpha(e)i).
\tag{V4}
\]

Gross–Tucker’s permutation-voltage theorem represents every finite graph covering in this manner.

A tower of regular voltage lifts with groups \(\Gamma_0,\ldots,\Gamma_{t-1}\) has exact size recurrence

\[
n_{i+1}=|\Gamma_i|\,n_i,
\qquad
n_t=n_0\prod_{i<t}|\Gamma_i|.
\tag{V5}
\]

**Why relevant.** The deck group at stage \(i+1\) is newly installed by the next voltage assignment; it need not descend from the previous diagonal quotient. Thus it is outside the lineage assumption. If Tanner checks are lifted equivariantly, the lifted code inherits the deck action.

**Main caveat.** Ordinary graph lifting expands length rather than folding it. It is useful only if paired with a balanced quotient or group-algebra product that spends the new deck group. A fixed star can be adjoined, but every invariant moving support is still a union of deck orbits.

**Verification path.** Use a small base Tanner graph, cyclic voltage groups \(\mathbb Z_3,\mathbb Z_5\), and build a tower

\[
B_0\leftarrow B_1\leftarrow B_2.
\]

Form a balanced product that quotients the first deck group and check whether the second deck group moves every nonstar quotient sector.

### 5. **Explicit abelian lifts and large quasi-cyclic symmetry**

**Source.** Fernando Granha Jeronimo, Tushant Mittal, Ryan O’Donnell, Pedro Paredes, and Madhur Tulsiani, “Explicit Abelian Lifts and Quantum LDPC Codes,” arXiv:2112.01647, 2021. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01647?utm_source=openai))

**Exact lift action.** An \((H,\ell)\)-lift replaces each base vertex \(v\) by

\[
(v,i),\qquad i\in[\ell],
\]

and an edge \(e=uv\), labelled by \(h_e\in H\leq S_\ell\), by the matching

\[
(u,i)\sim(v,h_e i).
\tag{AL1}
\]

For the regular cyclic action \(H=\mathbb Z_\ell\),

\[
a\cdot(v,i)=(v,i+a)
\tag{AL2}
\]

is free, so each nonidentity element has zero fixed lifted vertices.

The length recurrence is simply

\[
N_{\rm lift}=\ell N_{\rm base}.
\tag{AL3}
\]

**Precise code consequences.** Their Corollary 1.5 gives deterministic polynomial-time constructions of:

- good quasi-cyclic LDPC codes of block length \(N\) with circulant size up to \(N/\operatorname{polylog}N\), including size \(\Theta(N/\log N)\);
- quantum LDPC codes with
  \[
  d=\Omega(N/\log N),\qquad k=\Omega(\log N);
  \]
- for every constant \(\alpha>0\), quantum LDPC codes with
  \[
  d=\Omega(N^{1-\alpha}),\qquad k=\Theta(N^\alpha).
  \]

([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.01647))

**Why relevant.** This is an explicit source of extremely large cyclic coordinate symmetries without naively copying each base coordinate by a separately designed repetition gadget. It can feed either a balanced product or an orbit quotient.

**Invariant-word arithmetic.** For an \(\ell_0\)-quasi-cyclic code of length \(\ell_0m\), the generating shift has \(\ell_0\) disjoint cycles of length \(m\). Therefore:

\[
|\operatorname{Fix}_{\rm coordinates}(T^r)|=
\begin{cases}
\ell_0m,&r\equiv0\pmod m,\\
0,&r\not\equiv0\pmod m,
\end{cases}
\]

and every word invariant under the full shift has weight divisible by \(m\). Thus these constructions supply symmetry, but a sparse invariant witness requires \(m\) itself to be acceptable or requires invariance under a proper subgroup.

### 6. **Cyclic/quasi-cyclic product codes: exact serialization and exact mixed-word distance**

**Source.** Alexander Zeh and San Ling, “Spectral Analysis of Quasi-Cyclic Product Codes,” arXiv:1512.06690, 2015; extending Burton–Weldon’s cyclic product construction. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06690?utm_source=openai))

**Exact construction.** Let

\[
A=[n_A,k_A,d_A]_q,\qquad
B=[n_B,k_B,d_B]_q
\]

be respectively \(\ell_A\)- and \(\ell_B\)-quasi-cyclic, with \(\gcd(n_A,n_B)=1\). A product word is an \(n_B\times n_A\) matrix \(M=(m_{i,j})\) such that every row is in \(A\) and every column is in \(B\).

Choose \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\) satisfying

\[
a n_A+b n_B=1.
\]

The Chinese-remainder serialization is

\[
c(X)=
\sum_{i=0}^{n_B-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n_A-1}
m_{i,j}X^{\mu(i,j)}
\pmod{X^{n_An_B}-1},
\]

where

\[
\mu(i,j)
 =
 i\,a n_A+j\,b n_B
 \pmod{n_An_B}.
\tag{QC1}
\]

This satisfies \(\mu(i,j)\equiv i\pmod{n_B}\) and \(\mu(i,j)\equiv j\pmod{n_A}\). The resulting code is \(\ell_A\ell_B\)-quasi-cyclic with exact parameters

\[
[n_An_B,\ k_Ak_B,\ d_Ad_B]_q.
\tag{QC2}
\]

([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.06690))

**Why the distance statement is useful.** The proof of \(d=d_Ad_B\) is for an arbitrary nonzero product-code matrix, not only a rank-one or pure tensor word: a nonzero row has at least \(d_A\) nonzero columns, and each such nonzero column has weight at least \(d_B\).

**Symmetry count.** The quasi-cyclic shift by \(\ell_A\ell_B\) has order

\[
m_Am_B
 =
 \frac{n_An_B}{\ell_A\ell_B}.
\]

It has no fixed coordinate under a nonidentity power that generates a nonzero translation. A word invariant under the generating shift has weight divisible by \(m_Am_B\).

**Relevance and limitation.** This is an exact mixed-word distance theorem with a single cyclic coordinate index, but serialization is only a permutation of \(n_An_B\) coordinates—it does not compress length. It is most promising as an automorphism-rich factor inside a balanced product.

### 7. **Projection/gauge-fixing theorems for selected tensor-chain sectors**

**Source.** Weilei Zeng and Leonid P. Pryadko, “Minimal Distances for Certain Quantum Product Codes and Tensor Products of Chain Complexes,” 2020. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12152?utm_source=openai))

**Exact relevant construction.** For chain complexes \(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\), the degree-\(j\) tensor space decomposes as

\[
(\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B)_j
 =
 \bigoplus_r \mathcal A_r\otimes\mathcal B_{j-r}.
\]

They study projection to a single sector

\[
I=\mathcal A_i\otimes\mathcal B_{j-i}
\tag{ZP1}
\]

followed by puncturing the check matrices to \(I\) and gauge-fixing the resulting subsystem code. Their construction shows that the gauge-fixed stabilizer code has the same relevant \(Z\)-distance as the projected subsystem code.

In the terminal-boundary cases they obtain the exact product formula

\[
d_Z\bigl(\mathcal Q^{(i,j-i)}\bigr)
 =
 d_i(\mathcal A)\,d_{j-i}(\mathcal B)
\tag{ZP2}
\]

when \(\mathcal B_{j-i}\) is at the end of \(\mathcal B\); the same formula holds when \(\mathcal A_i\) is terminal. More generally, if one factor has only one nontrivial boundary operator, their theorem gives exact tensor-product homological distances. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12152))

**Why relevant to star-cross deletion.** This is the closest explicit pre-2024 theorem I found to deleting all but a selected tensor sector while retaining a distance product. It is not ordinary coordinate puncturing: projection is accompanied by a subsystem interpretation and gauge fixing. That extra quotient structure is exactly what prevents arbitrary deleted-sector cancellations.

**How to adapt.**

- Model the retained \((*,*)\) or moving-moving block as the selected subspace \(I\).
- Treat deleted star-cross sectors as gauge degrees of freedom rather than merely erasing coordinates.
- Check whether the pointed affine class corresponds to a nonzero logical component under the projection.
- The theorem applies directly only in terminal/one-boundary cases; a general pointed multi-sector analogue would need a new projection lemma.

### 8. **Rectangular puncturing/shortening of ordinary product codes: exact theorem, but star-cross retention is nonrectangular**

**Classical source.** Standard product-code theory, as used explicitly in the Burton–Weldon/Zeh–Ling product construction above. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.06690))

Let \(C\leq\mathbb F^X\), \(D\leq\mathbb F^Y\), and let \(S\subseteq X\), \(T\subseteq Y\).

Define coordinate restriction by \(\rho_S\) and shortening by

\[
\operatorname{Short}_S(C)
 =
 \{\rho_S(c):c\in C,\ \operatorname{supp}(c)\subseteq S\}.
\]

Then the exact rectangular identities are

\[
\rho_{S\times T}(C\otimes D)
 =
 \rho_S(C)\otimes\rho_T(D),
\tag{P1}
\]

and

\[
\operatorname{Short}_{S\times T}(C\otimes D)
 =
 \operatorname{Short}_S(C)\otimes
 \operatorname{Short}_T(D).
\tag{P2}
\]

Hence, whenever the factors are nonzero,

\[
d\!\left(\rho_{S\times T}(C\otimes D)\right)
 =
 d(\rho_S C)\,d(\rho_T D),
\tag{P3}
\]

and

\[
d\!\left(\operatorname{Short}_{S\times T}(C\otimes D)\right)
 =
 d(\operatorname{Short}_S C)\,
 d(\operatorname{Short}_T D).
\tag{P4}
\]

These hold for every mixed tensor-code word.

**But:** retaining

\[
((X\setminus P)\times(Y\setminus Q))\;\cup\;(P\times Q)
\tag{P5}
\]

while deleting the two cross rectangles

\[
P\times(Y\setminus Q),
\qquad
(X\setminus P)\times Q
\]

is not rectangular. The image generally couples the two retained diagonal blocks, so (P1)–(P4) do not imply a product-distance theorem.

For plain puncturing of any deleted set \(E\),

\[
d(\operatorname{Punct}_E C)\ge d(C)-|E|,
\tag{P6}
\]

and the same elementary inequality holds for pointed or relative distance if the distinguished coordinate is not deleted. Shortening cannot lower distance, but may remove the designated affine class entirely.

**Search conclusion on the requested star-cross theorem.** I did not find a classical theorem asserting an exact multiplicative distance after arbitrary deletion of the two star-cross sectors while retaining both the corner and moving-moving blocks. Zeng–Pryadko’s sector projection plus gauge fixing is the closest precise result. Without gauge conditions, arbitrary cross-sector cancellation appears to prevent a universal multiplicative statement.

## What is genuinely outside the supplied no-go assumptions?

### Clearly outside

1. **Chains of bimodules/bisets**
   \[
   U_0\times_{G_1}U_1\times_{G_2}\cdots
   \]
   with fresh right \(G_{i+1}\)-actions. The next action is not residual lineage from \(G_i\times G_i\).

2. **New voltage/deck groups installed after each quotient.** These are independent extension data, not subgroups of the old residual action.

3. **Gauge-fixed projection of selected tensor-chain sectors.** Deleted sectors are converted into gauge degrees of freedom rather than remaining fixed inherited coordinate sectors.

### Not by themselves outside enough

- A single cyclic or quasi-cyclic shift supplies large symmetry but forces invariant moving weight to be an orbit multiple.
- Ordinary cyclic product serialization preserves the full \(n_An_B\) length.
- A tower of graph lifts expands by \(\prod |G_i|\) unless each fresh deck group is subsequently spent through a balanced quotient.
- Balanced or lifted products alone do not guarantee a pointed sparse invariant YES word.
