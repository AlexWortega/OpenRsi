## No-go theorem: any incomplete family of global Walsh moments admits an exact all-eight-core virtual assignment

Let \(\Omega=\{0,1\}^n\), \(q=2^n\), and for \(T\subseteq[n]\) define the dense Walsh character
\[
\chi_T(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in T}x_i}.
\]
Fix a family \(\mathcal P\subseteq 2^{[n]}\setminus\{\varnothing\}\) of proposed global character rows.

Form the UNSAT constraint family \(\{C_u:u\in\Omega\}\), where \(C_u\) permits every global assignment except \(u\). For \(n=3\), these are exactly the eight width-three clauses, one forbidding each assignment.

For every group \(u\), introduce one column \((u,x)\) for each \(x\neq u\). The column represents the full global assignment \(x\), not a marginal or tableau view. Use:

* one coverage row for each \(u\), requiring
  \[
  \sum_{x\neq u}z_{u,x}=1;
  \]
* for every \(T\in\mathcal P\) and every \(u\neq 0^n\), one global-moment equality row
  \[
  \sum_{x\neq u}\chi_T(x)z_{u,x}
  -
  \sum_{x\neq 0^n}\chi_T(x)z_{0^n,x}=0.
  \]

Thus the matrix has
\[
N=q(q-1)
\]
columns and
\[
r=q+(q-1)|\mathcal P|
\]
rows. A common satisfying assignment, if one existed, would give the canonical one-column-per-group cost \(K=q\).

### Theorem

1. **Integer case.** If \(\mathcal P\) omits any nonempty Walsh character \(T_0\), then the above UNSAT system has an exact signed integer witness with
   \[
   |\operatorname{supp}z|=\|z\|_2^2=2q-2.
   \]
   Hence its cost is less than twice the canonical group baseline \(q\).

2. **GF(2) case.** If Walsh moments are represented by their parity bits
   \[
   b_T(x)=\sum_{i\in T}x_i\pmod 2,
   \]
   then for every \(n\ge2\), even including all \(2^n-1\) nonconstant parity characters leaves an exact witness of weight \(2q-2\).

These statements hold against arbitrary signed or GF(2) superpositions, not merely convex measures or pure assignment columns.

### Proof

Choose any anchor assignment \(a\in\Omega\). For every group \(u\neq a\), select the legal singleton \(x=a\):
\[
z_{u,a}=1.
\]

For the exceptional group \(u=a\), use every \(x\neq a\) with coefficient
\[
z_{a,x}
=
-\frac{\chi_{T_0}(x)}{\chi_{T_0}(a)}
\in\{-1,+1\}.
\tag{1}
\]

For every retained \(T\in\mathcal P\), Walsh orthogonality gives
\[
\sum_{x\in\Omega}\chi_{T_0}(x)\chi_T(x)=0,
\]
because \(T\neq T_0\). It also gives
\[
\sum_x\chi_{T_0}(x)=0
\]
because \(T_0\neq\varnothing\). Removing the term \(x=a\) and multiplying by \(-1/\chi_{T_0}(a)\) yields
\[
\sum_{x\neq a}z_{a,x}=1
\]
and
\[
\sum_{x\neq a}\chi_T(x)z_{a,x}=\chi_T(a).
\]
Thus every group has mass one and the same retained global moments as \(\delta_a\). All equations hold exactly. There are \(q-1\) ordinary singleton groups and \(q-1\) nonzero unit coefficients in the exceptional group, giving
\[
|\operatorname{supp}z|=\|z\|_2^2=(q-1)+(q-1)=2q-2.
\]

Over \(\mathbb F_2\), let
\[
w(x)=\bigl(1,(b_T(x))_{T\in\mathcal P}\bigr).
\]
For \(n\ge2\),
\[
\sum_{x\in\Omega}w(x)=0:
\]
the constant coordinate occurs \(2^n\) times, while each nonconstant parity is one on \(2^{n-1}\) assignments, both even. Therefore
\[
w(a)=\sum_{x\neq a}w(x).
\]
Using all \(x\neq a\) with coefficient one in the exceptional group gives the claimed binary witness.

Any row scaling, dense row mixing, residual-code postprocessing, or linear generation of these rows from another sparse dictionary preserves these exact identities.

### Sharpness over the integers

If all \(2^n-1\) nonconstant Walsh characters are retained, together with coverage, the complete Walsh transform is invertible over \(\mathbb Q\). Equal moments force every group to carry the same signed table \(\mu:\Omega\to\mathbb Z\). Since group \(u\) omits \(u\), it forces \(\mu(u)=0\). Applying this for every \(u\) gives \(\mu=0\), contradicting mass one.

Thus the integer threshold is exact: this core is feasible precisely when at least one nonconstant Walsh character is missing. Full global Fourier closure requires \(2^n\) independent rows.

The GF(2) conclusion is stronger because the Walsh transform degenerates in characteristic two; adding every parity character still does not recover full truth-table information.

### Explicit all-eight hostile test

Take \(n=3\), so
\[
q=8,\qquad N=8\cdot7=56,\qquad
r=8+7|\mathcal P|.
\]
For every proper subset
\[
\mathcal P\subsetneq
\bigl\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{1,2,3\}\bigr\},
\]
and every anchor \(a\in\{0,1\}^3\):

1. choose any omitted \(T_0\);
2. build the witness from (1);
3. verify exactly over \(\mathbb Z\) that \(Az=t\);
4. verify
   \[
   |\operatorname{supp}z|=\|z\|_2^2=14;
   \]
5. reduce the parity-bit matrix modulo two and verify the all-plus exceptional table also has syndrome \(t\) and weight \(14\);
6. multiply rows by deterministic dense integer and binary matrices and recheck the identities.

For an exhaustive minimum-cost check, enumerate each group’s \(3^7\) coefficient vectors in \(\{-1,0,1\}^7\) having sum one, hash them by their moment tuple, retain minimum squared norm per tuple, and intersect the eight hash tables. For GF(2), enumerate the \(2^7\) local subsets, retain those of odd cardinality, and hash by their parity-moment tuple. Finally verify that with all seven integer characters present, the exact fiber is empty by rational row reduction.

The support-three circuit fault is the \(n=2\) instance of the same mechanism when the quadratic character is omitted. Mixed \(2/3\) virtual measures offer no defense: they are merely additional signed linear combinations, while the displayed witness already lies in the exact kernel with unit coefficients.

There are no unproved lemmas. The theorem does not rule out an integer construction that supplies the complete \(2^n\)-row Walsh basis on every genuinely global \(n\)-bit object; it proves that any incomplete character family fails exactly, even on the smallest all-eight core, and that GF(2) Walsh/parity characters fail even when complete.
