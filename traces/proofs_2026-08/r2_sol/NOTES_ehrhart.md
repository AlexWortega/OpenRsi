## Immediate run

The differential-evolution tetrahedron run made no progress after four minutes and was terminated; its discontinuous penalty was a poor optimizer objective. Also ran `random_center_scale_polytopes.py` on 10,000 random six-vertex polytopes: best scaled volume was only `5.699`, far below `32/3`. A second nudge, hill climbing from the sharp simplex while allowing two extra vertices, returned either the exact sharp simplex (extra points inside it) or volume `10.558<32/3`. These are heuristic diagnostics only and prove nothing; the continuous optimizer route is now banked as stalled after two nudges. Adversarial code review found a crucial limitation: `optimize_tetrahedra.py` enforces the centroid by averaging vertices, valid for tetrahedra but invalid for general polytopes; the random/hill scripts instead compute the volume centroid by tetrahedral decomposition. The first exact-enumeration nudge instead used integer vertices (cheaper than facet normals): all centroid-zero integer tetrahedra in `[-1,3]^3` were exhaustively checked. Exactly 1,078 are nondegenerate and interior-lattice-free, and the unique volume maximizer is the sharp simplex with determinant 64, volume `32/3`. `verify_integer_tetrahedra_box.py` independently repeats the enumeration and passes. This is a finite boxed benchmark only, not a theorem for arbitrary tetrahedra or bodies. A second exact enumeration enlarged to `[-2,4]^3`: among 135,534 ordered centroid-zero candidate quadruples, 26,928 are nondegenerate and interior-lattice-free; all have volume at most `32/3`. There are 19 equality coordinate representatives, and exact edge-matrix checks show each has Smith factors `(4,4,4)`, hence is unimodularly equivalent to the sharp simplex. `verify_integer_tetrahedra_box_2_4.py` passes. This reinforces the extremizer pattern but is still boxed integer data. A denominator-2 enumeration in `[-1,2]^3` found maximum volume only `14/3`; because that asymmetric box excludes the sharp simplex coordinate range and the result currently lacks an independent verifier, it is only a search diagnostic, not a banked claim. The wider denominator-2 exhaustive box produced no progress output after more than 17 CPU-minutes and was terminated. This is the second low-yield enumeration nudge, so rational box enumeration is banked as stalled rather than scaled further.

A new exact arithmetic route emerged from replacing coordinate boxes by Hermite normal forms. `experiments/enumerate_centered_hnf_tetrahedra.py` enumerates centered integer-vertex tetrahedra by edge-lattice HNF and tests interior lattice points through exact quotient residues. Through determinant 70, 229,510 HNFs were tested, 3,268 were centered, and 62 had only the origin in the interior; the unique maximum is `4I` with determinant 64. `verify_centered_hnf_70.py` independently repeats this finite computation and passes. A search through determinant 200 found no additional feasible HNF, but that larger result is not promoted because the independent verifier intentionally stops at 70.

More importantly, the quotient formulation exposed a short general argument. For an integer-vertex `n`-simplex with edge matrix `A`, barycenter zero means `q=(1/(n+1),...,1/(n+1))` lies in `A^{-1}Z^n/Z^n`. If `|det A|>(n+1)^n`, pigeonholing that finite group into side-`1/(n+1)` cubes gives a nonzero group element `d` with every `|d_i|<1/(n+1)`; choosing its sign so `sum d_i<=0` puts `q+d` strictly inside the standard simplex and yields a nonzero interior lattice point. Therefore `|det A|<=(n+1)^n`. This proves the target for lattice-vertex simplices (a subclass of the known simplex case) and is written in `proof_ehrhart.md`. Hostile checks: the small-cube difference is nonzero on the torus; its coordinates lie strictly in `(-1/(n+1),1/(n+1))`; `A d` is integral; and invertibility makes it nonzero.

A separate symmetric-core diagnostic sampled 10,000 vertex-generated, then 50,000 five-vertex, and 20,000 independently facet-generated volume-centered polytopes. Every run found `vol(K cap -K)/vol(K)>=1/2` numerically, with equality at the sharp 3-simplex and many simplex-like or degenerate samples. This strongly suggests the dimension-three inequality `vol(K cap -K)>=vol(K)/2`; if true, Minkowski would give only `vol(K)<=16`, not the target `32/3`. Two direct 200,000-step simulated-annealing runs from the simplex, with six and eight vertices, also stopped at `1/2` to `1.6e-15`. This second nudge is complete. The symmetric-core numerical route is banked: it strongly points to a clean `vol(K)<=16` partial bound if the suspected geometric inequality is proved, but cannot reach the target by itself. Thus it is potentially a clean improvement over `4^3=64` but cannot by itself settle dimension three. No theorem is claimed from the numerical experiment. A targeted centered-pyramid sample initially crashed because `numpy.cross` no longer accepts 2D vectors; after fixing the determinant explicitly, 20,000 samples again minimized at triangular bases with exact tetrahedral ratio `1/2`. Regular 4–10-gonal centered pyramids had ratios around `0.584–0.594`. This is further heuristic support that simplices minimize the symmetric core, not a proof. An exhaustive numerical benchmark over all convex 4- and 5-point integer bases in `[-2,2]^2` (19,026 centered pyramids) found minimum core ratio `0.5076734>1/2`; `verify_pyramid_core_box2.py` independently reproduces it. Because floating geometry is used, this is explicitly only a numerical finite benchmark, not a rigorous exact inequality. A separate 5,000-sample integer-hull reduction test failed decisively: high-volume first-contact bodies often contained only the origin plus one boundary lattice point, so their lattice-point hull had zero three-volume. Thus reduction to the convex hull of lattice points is banked as unusable without a new thickening argument. A 20,000-sample polar-duality diagnostic found volume product `vol(K)vol(K^o)>=64/9≈7.111` among the 100 highest-volume samples, with the sharp simplex attaining exactly `64/9`; this is consistent with the classical simplex-minimizes-volume-product phenomenon but supplies no lattice control on the polar, hence no new proof route.

The final pyramid nudge isolated a precise planar inequality rather than another undirected search. For a centroid-zero planar body `B`, centered-pyramid horizontal core sections are `uB cap (-vB)` with `u=(3-z)/4`, `v=(3+z)/4`, `|z|<=1`. In 30,000 random bases, the minimum normalized intersection area at every one of 17 sampled heights was attained by a triangular base and matched `(3-z^2)/8`; integrating this formula gives the tetrahedral core ratio `1/2`. Thus proving

`area(uB cap -vB)/area(B) >= (3-z^2)/8`

for every centroid-zero planar `B` would establish `vol(K cap -K)>=vol(K)/2` for every centered 3D pyramid, hence the lattice-free partial bound `vol(K)<=16` for that class. The missing planar inequality is not proved here. `verify_triangle_scaled_intersection.py` checks only the triangular formula/integral, not the universal inequality.

The next route was a corrected directional-certificate stress test. `experiments/stress_directional_certificate.py` sampled 3,000 genuinely volume-centroided seven-vertex polytopes, scaled them to first lattice contact, and measured `width_u rho_u^2` over small primitive directions. The sharp simplex calibration has best sampled score `4` (direction `(0,1,1)`, symmetric zero section), still above the proved threshold `8/3`. The best random body had volume only `5.319` and score about `5.999`; only one of the top 100 random-volume records met the existing certificate. Thus the experiment confirms that the certificate misses most asymmetric samples, but random sampling does not reach the near-extremal regime and reveals no credible new inequality. This route is diagnostic only and is not promoted to a finite theorem.
