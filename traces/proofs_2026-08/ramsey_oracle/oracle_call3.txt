## 1. Matching number at most five does **not** make the family too small

Fix an anchor \(a\). Put

\[
U=[g]\setminus\{0,a\},\qquad n=|U|=g-2,\qquad k=r-1,
\]

so the vertices over \(a\) are indexed by \(B\in\binom Uk\), with palette

\[
P(a,B)=\{0,a\}\cup B.
\]

Let \(\mathcal F_a\subseteq\binom Uk\) be the retained family. The six-disjoint-sets argument proves the necessary condition

\[
\nu(\mathcal F_a)\le 5.
\]

For \(g=\lfloor r^2\rfloor\), this condition costs only a polynomial factor.

### Exact large-\(n\) matching bound

The large-\(n\) Erdős matching theorem states that, if

\[
n\ge (2s+1)k-s,
\]

then every \(\mathcal F\subseteq\binom{[n]}k\) with \(\nu(\mathcal F)\le s\) satisfies

\[
|\mathcal F|
\le
\binom nk-\binom{n-s}k.
\]

For \(s=5\), \(n=r^2-2\), and \(k=r-1\), the hypothesis is

\[
r^2-2\ge 11(r-1)-5,
\]

which holds for every \(r\ge 10\). Hence

\[
|\mathcal F_a|
\le
M_r:=
\binom{r^2-2}{r-1}
-
\binom{r^2-7}{r-1}.
\]

This is attained by choosing a fixed \(5\)-set \(S\subset U\) and taking

\[
\mathcal F_S=\{B\in\textstyle\binom Uk:B\cap S\neq\varnothing\}.
\]

Indeed, six pairwise-disjoint members would require six distinct elements of \(S\).

Moreover,

\[
\frac{M_r}{\binom nk}
=
1-\frac{\binom{n-5}k}{\binom nk}
=
1-\prod_{j=0}^{4}\left(1-\frac{k}{n-j}\right)
=
\frac5r+O(r^{-2}).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\log M_r
=
r\log r+r+O(\log r).
\]

Multiplying by the \(\Theta(g)=\Theta(r^2)\) choices of anchor changes only the \(O(\log r)\) term. Since every palette has size \(r+1\), the matching-number restriction still permits

\[
\log N=(1+o(1))\,r\log r.
\]

Thus:

> **Quantitative verdict:** the matching-number obstruction does not kill the anchored-palette route on cardinality grounds. It removes only a \(\Theta(1/r)\) fraction from the full family.

## 2. The Erdős-matching extremizer is nevertheless impossible

Matching number controls only sunflowers with empty core. Nonempty cores give stronger Ramsey obstructions.

### Link-matching/Ramsey lemma

For \(C\subseteq U\), define the link

\[
\mathcal L_{\mathcal F}(C)
=
\{B\setminus C:B\in\mathcal F,\ C\subseteq B\}.
\]

If a legal triangle-free coloring exists over the fixed anchor \(a\), then necessarily

\[
\boxed{\quad
\nu\bigl(\mathcal L_{\mathcal F}(C)\bigr)
\le R_{|C|+2}(3)-1
\quad}
\tag{1}
\]

for every \(C\subseteq U\).

#### Proof

Suppose instead that \(q=R_{|C|+2}(3)\) members of the link are pairwise disjoint. Write them as \(Q_1,\dots,Q_q\), and let

\[
B_i=C\cup Q_i.
\]

Then \(B_i\cap B_j=C\) for \(i\ne j\). Hence

\[
P(a,B_i)\cap P(a,B_j)=\{0,a\}\cup C.
\]

Every edge among these \(q\) vertices must therefore receive one of exactly \(|C|+2\) colors. By the definition of \(R_{|C|+2}(3)\), the induced \(K_q\) contains a monochromatic triangle, a contradiction. ∎

The original six-set obstruction is exactly the case \(C=\varnothing\), because \(R_2(3)=6\).

### Failure of the maximum matching family

Take the extremal family

\[
\mathcal F_S=\{B:B\cap S\neq\varnothing\},\qquad |S|=5.
\]

Fix \(x\in S\). If

\[
r^2-7\ge 17(r-2),
\]

we can choose pairwise-disjoint \((r-2)\)-sets

\[
Q_1,\dots,Q_{17}\subseteq U\setminus S
\]

and set \(B_i=\{x\}\cup Q_i\). The inequality holds for every \(r\ge16\). Then

\[
B_i\cap B_j=\{x\},
\]

so all edges among the corresponding 17 vertices have only the three legal colors

\[
\{0,a,x\}.
\]

Since \(R_3(3)=17\), this is impossible.

Therefore the exact maximum-size matching salvage is not colorable for \(r\ge16\).

More generally, every admissible retained family must satisfy the entire hierarchy (1), not merely its \(C=\varnothing\) instance.

## 3. Even the full link-matching hierarchy does not force small families

The hierarchy above is a genuine obstruction, but it still does not settle the route quantitatively.

Let

\[
t=\lceil \log_2 n\rceil
\]

and fix \(T\subset U\) with \(|T|=t\). Consider

\[
\mathcal F_T=\{B\in\textstyle\binom Uk:T\subseteq B\}.
\]

Then

\[
|\mathcal F_T|=\binom{n-t}{k-t}
\]

and, because \(t=O(\log r)\),

\[
\log |\mathcal F_T|
=
(1-o(1))\,r\log r.
\]

Moreover, \(\mathcal F_T\) satisfies every necessary condition (1):

* If \(T\nsubseteq C\), every member of \(\mathcal L_{\mathcal F_T}(C)\) contains the fixed nonempty set \(T\setminus C\), so its matching number is at most one.
* If \(T\subseteq C\), its matching number is at most \(n\).
* The standard first-difference coloring of binary strings gives
  \[
  R_m(3)>2^m.
  \]
  Hence, for \(|C|\ge t\),
  \[
  R_{|C|+2}(3)>2^{t+2}>n.
  \]

Thus a common core of only \(O(\log r)\) colors evades all sunflower/link-matching Ramsey obstructions while losing only \(o(r\log r)\) entropy.

This does **not** give a coloring. It shows precisely why matching and sunflower accounting alone cannot finish the problem: after concentrating the family around a logarithmic core, one still needs a genuinely correlated rule using the varying petal colors to cover every edge while keeping each color class triangle-free.

## Final verdict

I do not have a viable construction with such a rule, so I do not propose another candidate.

The sharp lesson is:

> The matching-number condition is quantitatively harmless but structurally far too weak. A legal anchored family must satisfy the link constraints  
> \[
> \nu(\mathcal L_{\mathcal F}(C))<R_{|C|+2}(3)
> \]
> for every core \(C\). These constraints kill the Erdős-matching extremizers, yet logarithmic-core families of size \(\exp((1-o(1))r\log r)\) satisfy all of them. Therefore further set-family pruning, without an explicit edge-coloring mechanism, cannot constitute a salvage.

The stationary-automaton experiments likewise provide no rigorous asymptotic obstruction, but there is currently no proved synchronization mechanism to replace the failed finite searches. Hence neither experimental route presently supplies a defensible growing-base construction.
