## Theorem

Let \(H\) be any triangle-free graph on \(n\) vertices, and let

\[
M_{uv}=\mathbf 1[u=v\ \text{or}\ uv\notin E(H)].
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{per}(M)\ge \frac{n!}{C^n}},
\qquad
C=\frac52\left(\frac53\right)^{3/2}
=\frac{25\sqrt{15}}{18}<5.38.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\left(\frac{n!}{\operatorname{per}(M)}\right)^{1/n}<5.38
\]

for every triangle-free \(H\). Thus the permanent-quotient guarantee can never have growing base, and in particular no family can satisfy

\[
\operatorname{per}(M)\le \frac{n!}{n^{cn}}
\]

for any fixed \(c>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\).

The proof has two ingredients.

---

## 1. A dense bipartite support has a large regular factor

### Lemma 1

Let \(G=(L,R;E)\) be bipartite with \(|L|=|R|=m\). Suppose every vertex has at most \(a\) nonneighbors on the other side, where

\[
a\le \frac{4m}{9}.
\]

Then \(G\) contains a spanning \(r\)-regular subgraph with

\[
r=m-2a.
\]

### Proof

By the integral max-flow criterion, an \(r\)-factor exists if and only if, for every \(X\subseteq L\) and \(Y\subseteq R\),

\[
e(X,R\setminus Y)\ge r(|X|-|Y|).
\tag{1}
\]

Write \(x=|X|\), \(y=|Y|\). There is nothing to prove when \(x\le y\), so assume \(x>y\).

The rectangle \(X\times(R\setminus Y)\) contains \(x(m-y)\) pairs. Its number of nonedges is at most both \(ax\) and \(a(m-y)\). Therefore

\[
e(X,R\setminus Y)
\ge x(m-y)-\min\{ax,a(m-y)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

First suppose \(x\le m-y\). Then (2) gives

\[
e(X,R\setminus Y)\ge x(m-y-a).
\]

Subtracting \(r(x-y)=(m-2a)(x-y)\) yields

\[
x(a-y)+(m-2a)y.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(y\le a\), this is nonnegative. If \(y>a\), then \(a-y<0\) and \(x\le m-y\), so (3) is at least

\[
(m-y)(a-y)+(m-2a)y
= y^2-3ay+am.
\tag{4}
\]

The discriminant of the quadratic in (4) is

\[
9a^2-4am\le 0
\]

because \(a\le 4m/9\). Hence (4) is nonnegative.

Now suppose \(x\ge m-y\). From (2),

\[
e(X,R\setminus Y)\ge (m-y)(x-a).
\]

After subtracting \(r(x-y)\), the result is

\[
x(2a-y)+(m-a)y-am.
\tag{5}
\]

If \(y\le2a\), expression (5) is increasing in \(x\), so using \(x\ge m-y\) gives again

\[
(5)\ge y^2-3ay+am\ge0.
\]

If \(y\ge2a\), expression (5) is decreasing in \(x\), so using \(x\le m\) gives

\[
(5)\ge a(m-y)\ge0.
\]

Thus (1) always holds, proving the existence of the \(r\)-factor. ∎

### Corollary 2

Let \(A\) be an \(m\times m\) \(0\)-\(1\) matrix with at most \(a\le4m/9\) zeros in every row and column. Then

\[
\operatorname{per}(A)
\ge m!\left(1-\frac{2a}{m}\right)^m.
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, Lemma 1 gives an \(r\)-regular support matrix \(B\le A\), \(r=m-2a\). Since \(B/r\) is doubly stochastic, van der Waerden gives

\[
\operatorname{per}(B/r)\ge \frac{m!}{m^m}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{per}(A)\ge\operatorname{per}(B)
\ge r^m\frac{m!}{m^m}
=m!\left(1-\frac{2a}{m}\right)^m.
\]

## 2. Recursively peel independent neighborhoods

Fix

\[
\beta=\frac25.
\]

Start with \(H_0=H\), on a residual vertex set of size \(m_0=n\).

As long as the current induced graph \(H_i\), of order \(m_i\), has a vertex \(v_i\) with

\[
d_{H_i}(v_i)>\beta m_i,
\]

remove the block

\[
B_i=N_{H_i}(v_i),
\qquad b_i=|B_i|.
\]

Because \(H_i\) is triangle-free, \(B_i\) is independent. Hence every entry of \(M\) in \(B_i\times B_i\) equals \(1\). The residual size becomes

\[
m_{i+1}=m_i-b_i.
\]

Eventually this terminates with a residual set \(T\) of size \(t\), whose induced graph satisfies

\[
\Delta(H[T])\le\beta t=\frac{2t}{5}.
\]

For the reflexive nonadjacency matrix \(M_T\), every row and column has at most \(2t/5\) zeros. Corollary 2 applies because \(2/5<4/9\), giving

\[
\operatorname{per}(M_T)
\ge t!\left(1-\frac45\right)^t
=\frac{t!}{5^t}.
\tag{7}
\]

Restricting to permutations that preserve every block \(B_i\) and preserve \(T\), we obtain

\[
\operatorname{per}(M)
\ge
\left(\prod_i b_i!\right)\operatorname{per}(M_T)
\ge
\left(\prod_i b_i!\right)\frac{t!}{5^t}.
\tag{8}
\]

It remains to compare this product of factorials with \(n!\).

---

## 3. Entropy estimate for the peeled block sizes

Let

\[
p_i=\frac{b_i}{n},
\qquad
p_*=\frac tn.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{n!}{\left(\prod_i b_i!\right)t!}
\le
\exp\!\left(nH(p_1,p_2,\dots,p_*)\right),
\tag{9}
\]

where \(H\) is Shannon entropy with natural logarithms.

Set

\[
x_i=\frac{b_i}{m_i}.
\]

By construction \(x_i>\beta=2/5\). The entropy chain rule gives

\[
H(p_1,p_2,\dots,p_*)
=
\sum_i \frac{m_i}{n}h(x_i),
\tag{10}
\]

where

\[
h(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x).
\]

The function \(h(x)/x\) is strictly decreasing on \((0,1)\), since

\[
\frac{d}{dx}\frac{h(x)}x
=\frac{\log(1-x)}{x^2}<0.
\]

Thus, for every \(x_i\ge2/5\),

\[
h(x_i)\le F x_i,
\qquad
F=\frac{h(2/5)}{2/5}.
\]

Since \((m_i/n)x_i=b_i/n\),

\[
H(p_1,p_2,\dots,p_*)
\le
F\sum_i\frac{b_i}{n}
=F(1-p_*).
\tag{11}
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
F
=
-\log\frac25-\frac32\log\frac35
=
\log\left[\frac52\left(\frac53\right)^{3/2}\right]
=\log C.
\tag{12}
\]

Also \(C>5\), so \(\log5<\log C=F\). Combining (8)–(12),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{n!}{\operatorname{per}(M)}
&\le
nH(p_1,p_2,\dots,p_*)+t\log5\\
&\le
nF(1-p_*)+np_*\log5\\
&\le nF.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{per}(M)\ge n!e^{-nF}
=\frac{n!}{C^n},
\]

as claimed.

## Consequences for the permutation-orbit construction

For the bad-pair degree

\[
D=\operatorname{per}(M),
\]

the quotient used by the transitive greedy argument always satisfies

\[
\frac{n!}{D}\le C^n,
\qquad C<5.38.
\]

Hence its achieved base is universally bounded:

\[
\left(\frac{n!}{D}\right)^{1/n}<5.38.
\]

In particular, an explicit classical or pseudorandom triangle-free family cannot make \(D\) smaller by \(n^{\Omega(n)}\); the universal lower bound rules this out.

This conclusion applies specifically to the permanent/degree quotient \(n!/D\). It does not prove that every separated subset of the permutation space has size at most \(C^n\): the bad-pair graph could conceivably have an independent set much larger than its elementary degree bound. Such an improvement would require additional structure beyond the permanent mechanism.

For scale, balanced \(H=K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}\) has

\[
D=\lfloor n/2\rfloor!\,\lceil n/2\rceil!,
\qquad
\frac{n!}{D}=\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sim \frac{2^n}{\sqrt{\pi n/2}},
\]

so any optimal universal constant in this type of theorem is at least \(2\).
