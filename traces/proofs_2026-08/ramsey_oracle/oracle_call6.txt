I cannot honestly provide either requested resolution: the permutation-family problem is asymptotically equivalent to the unresolved question of whether multicolor triangle Ramsey numbers have a universal exponential upper bound. The equivalence is stronger than the one-way implication stated in the update.

## 1. Constant-composition blow-up lemma

For a triangle-free graph \(H\), let \(s_H(m)\) be the maximum size of a family
\[
C\subseteq V(H)^m
\]
such that every distinct \(x,y\in C\) satisfy \(x_i y_i\in E(H)\) for some coordinate \(i\).

### Lemma
Let \(H\) have \(q\) vertices, and let \(C\subseteq V(H)^m\) be \(H\)-separated. Then there is a triangle-free graph \(J\) on exactly \(m\) vertices and a \(J\)-separated permutation family \(\mathcal F\subseteq S_m\) with
\[
|\mathcal F|
\ge
\frac{|C|}{\binom{m+q-1}{q-1}}
\ge
\frac{|C|}{(m+1)^q}.
\]

### Proof
Every word \(x\in V(H)^m\) has a composition vector
\[
r(x)=(r_v(x))_{v\in V(H)},\qquad
r_v(x)=|\{i:x_i=v\}|.
\]
There are at most \(\binom{m+q-1}{q-1}\) possible compositions. Hence some subfamily \(C'\subseteq C\) has constant composition \(r=(r_v)\) and
\[
|C'|\ge |C|/\binom{m+q-1}{q-1}.
\]

Define the blow-up
\[
V(J)=\{(v,a):v\in V(H),\ 1\le a\le r_v\},
\]
and put
\[
(v,a)(w,b)\in E(J)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad vw\in E(H).
\]
There are no edges within a clone class. Any triangle in \(J\) would therefore project to a triangle in \(H\), so \(J\) is triangle-free. Also
\[
|V(J)|=\sum_v r_v=m.
\]

For \(x\in C'\), define
\[
\pi_x(i)=\bigl(x_i,\;|\{j\le i:x_j=x_i\}|\bigr).
\]
Because \(x\) has composition \(r\), this is a bijection from \([m]\) to \(V(J)\), hence a permutation after fixing an arbitrary labeling of \(V(J)\) by \([m]\).

If \(x\ne y\), choose \(i\) with \(x_i y_i\in E(H)\). Then \(\pi_x(i)\pi_y(i)\in E(J)\). Thus \(\{\pi_x:x\in C'\}\) is \(J\)-separated. ∎

This construction is directly implementable: group codewords by their symbol-count vector, create one clone per occurrence count, and label the \(a\)-th occurrence of each symbol by clone \(a\).

## 2. Exact asymptotic equivalence

Define
\[
P(n)=
\max_{\substack{J\text{ triangle-free}\\ |V(J)|=n}}
\max\{|\mathcal F|:\mathcal F\subseteq S_n
\text{ is }J\text{-separated}\}.
\]
Also define the separated capacity
\[
\Theta_{\rm sep}(H)=\sup_{m\ge1}s_H(m)^{1/m}.
\]

### Theorem
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{n\to\infty}P(n)^{1/n}
=
\sup_{H\text{ finite triangle-free}}\Theta_{\rm sep}(H).
}
\]

### Proof
A \(J\)-separated permutation family is, after forgetting that its words are permutations, a \(J\)-separated subset of \(V(J)^n\). Hence
\[
P(n)^{1/n}\le
\sup_H\Theta_{\rm sep}(H).
\]

Conversely, fix a finite triangle-free \(H\) with \(q\) vertices. Applying the lemma to a maximum \(H\)-separated code in length \(m\),
\[
P(m)\ge
\frac{s_H(m)}{(m+1)^q}.
\]
Taking \(m\)-th roots and then the limsup gives
\[
\limsup_m P(m)^{1/m}\ge\Theta_{\rm sep}(H),
\]
because \((m+1)^{q/m}\to1\). Now take the supremum over \(H\). ∎

Using the exact strong-power formulation from the brief,
\[
R_k(3)-1=\max_{H\text{ triangle-free}}s_H(k).
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\sup_H\Theta_{\rm sep}(H)
=
\sup_{k\ge1}(R_k(3)-1)^{1/k}.
}
\]
Indeed, the left side is at most the right side because \(s_H(k)\le R_k(3)-1\). Conversely, a graph attaining \(R_k(3)-1\) in power \(k\) has capacity at least \((R_k(3)-1)^{1/k}\).

Consequently:

* A universal bound \(P(n)\le C^n\) is equivalent to
  \[
  R_k(3)\le C^k+1\qquad\text{for every }k.
  \]
* Conversely, any universal exponential Ramsey upper bound immediately gives the same permutation-family upper bound.
* Thus permutation balance does not asymptotically make the zero-error problem easier: after allowing triangle-free blow-ups, arbitrary separated codes can be made into permutations with only a subexponential loss.

## 3. The proposed fractional bad-clique cover cannot prove an exponential bound

Call \(Q\subseteq S_n\) a bad clique if every pair \(\pi,\sigma\in Q\) is coordinatewise equal-or-nonadjacent in \(H\).

For each coordinate \(i\), define
\[
A_i=\{\pi(i):\pi\in Q\}.
\]
Then \(A_i\) is independent in \(H\): if \(u,v\in A_i\) were adjacent, permutations taking values \(u,v\) at position \(i\) would not be a bad pair. Hence
\[
|Q|\le\prod_{i=1}^n|A_i|\le\alpha(H)^n.
\]

Now suppose bad cliques \(Q\) have nonnegative weights \(w_Q\) and fractionally cover \(S_n\):
\[
\sum_{Q\ni\pi}w_Q\ge1
\qquad\forall\pi\in S_n.
\]
Summing over \(\pi\),
\[
n!
\le
\sum_Q w_Q|Q|
\le
\alpha(H)^n\sum_Qw_Q.
\]
Thus every such cover has total weight at least
\[
\boxed{
\sum_Qw_Q\ge \frac{n!}{\alpha(H)^n}.
}
\]

There are triangle-free graphs with \(\alpha(H)=o(n)\); an elementary random construction gives, for example,
\[
\alpha(H)=O(n^{2/3}\log n).
\]
For these graphs,
\[
\frac{n!}{\alpha(H)^n}
\ge
\left(\frac{c\,n^{1/3}}{\log n}\right)^n,
\]
which is superexponential. Therefore no fractional cover by bad cliques—or by coordinate cylinders contained in bad cliques—can have universal exponential total weight.

So the natural fractional-cover hope in the update is ruled out, not merely unfinished.

## Conclusion

The maximum separated permutation-family question is not presently a tractable specialization: via constant-composition blow-ups, a universal exponential upper bound is equivalent to the longstanding universal exponential upper bound for \(R_k(3)\). The most natural proposed proof mechanism, an exponential-weight fractional cover by bad cliques or independent-coordinate cylinders, is impossible for triangle-free graphs with sublinear independence number. I do not have a valid universal exponential proof or a superexponential explicit construction.
